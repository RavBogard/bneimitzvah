### God-language and the theology of prayer

**C1.** > This God that is an unchanging oneness, truth of the universe… a moment to take a cosmic perspective instead of a human perspective… awe and transcendence at our existential smallness. That's the kind of theology I want throughout.
— `planning/VOICE-THEOLOGY-CHARTER-2026-08-16.md:33-35` · AUTHOR: Daniel (charter quote)

**C2.** > "It's the prayers, the liturgy that we need to change the theology of, not the biblical pieces." · "Liturgies don't need to be straight translated, but Torah does."
— `planning/VOICE-THEOLOGY-CHARTER-2026-08-16.md:16-17` · AUTHOR: Daniel (charter quote)

**C3.** > We each walk into the holidays with our own conception of God, or with none at all. For some of us, God is just another word for the unknowable Truth of Creation; for others, God is more traditional, more personal and approachable; and for many of us (according to the surveys, at least), humanistic language feels the most comfortable and authentic. So this book varies how it names the Divine, from the Eternal and the Holy One to HaShem and simply You, with the goal being that each of us should be able to see ourselves within the text.
— `build/typst/content/rosh-hashanah/frontmatter.typ:200-206` · AUTHOR: Daniel (his signed Editor's Note; "his approved text VERBATIM, iterated in chat over five renders", source comment :150-153) · Surface: machzor front matter (printed)

**C4.** > the amidah [is] fundamentally a set of blessings around the gap between the world as it is and the world we are obliged to create.

**C5.** > it would feel cringe if it was everywhere.

It's the plea that is a howl and a shriek and a visceral scream that this isn't ok, that it must be different, when we feel so powerless to control that which is controlling us.
