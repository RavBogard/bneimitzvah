# A3 voice model: predicting Daniel's teaching on a prayer

Version 0, 2026-10-24. Built from `A3-INDEX.md` (C1–C39) and the per-prayer files. Each rule cites its evidence. Every approval, edit or pop-up answer adds a line to the log at the bottom and, where it changes a rule, edits the rule. The goal (Daniel, 2026-10-24): "an evolving algorithm that can predict my next teaching on a prayer in my voice, progressively."

## Stance

1. **Humanistic first, with room for theists** (Daniel, 2026-10-24: "Always a humanistic approach that leaves room for more theistic people to still resonate").
2. **Lead humanistic** (Daniel, 2026-10-24, chose this over naming both readings side by side). Give the humanistic reading as the main line; let "God" stand as a name for it ("Jews have long called that oneness God") so a theist still finds a home, without a "for others" paragraph. **Keep God in the prayer; widen what the word can mean.** The Sh'ma "can't be translated without God in it" (C-11.3). Deleting a divine title is "absolutely disqualifying" (C6). The move is the Editor's Note's: "For some of us, God is just another word for the unknowable Truth of Creation; for others, God is more traditional…" (C3).
3. **Cosmic smallness, not divine command.** "unchanging oneness, truth of the universe… awe and transcendence at our existential smallness" (C1); "a piece in a much larger puzzle, rather than the purpose of it all" (C7).
4. **Prayer turns toward the world we are obliged to make.** The Amidah is "the gap between the world as it is and the world we are obliged to create" (C4); love means "mutually obligated… most of all, to the future" (C25); Shabbat rest for everyone (C26).
5. **Honest about hard text.** Read power-language as "a fantasy of power in times of powerlessness" (C27); reward and punishment as consequence, not threat (V'ahavta notes, uncertain authorship).
6. **Everyone sees themselves.** "non-binary people, queer people, people who weren't born Jewish" (C23); "The book holds all of us" (C39, uncertain).

## Shape of a teaching

7. **Voice: the teacher's note** (Daniel's pick, 2026-10-24, over spoken and spare voices). Measured, explanatory, "the academic note tone" (C35): what the prayer is, a source or two, then what it asks of us. Images (spokes toward a center, C-08.1) are welcome inside the explanation, not as an opening hook.
8. **Then what the prayer is and where it sits**, briefly: "structural & educational (what a prayer is, where it sits in the frame, brief history)" (C38).
9. **One learned detail at most**, in "the academic note tone" (C35), "educational, not 'na na na'" (C36).
10. **Short.** His meaning statements run 1–4 sentences; his captions end hard ("End it there. Period.", C-08.3). Target for the course: 80–160 words. [Inferred; check with Daniel]
11. **"Some of us…" for practice**: "inherited practice freely offered, zero prescription" (C34). Never tell the reader what to believe or do.

## Words

12. Titles transliterated, not translated (C17).
13. Never "worship"/"worshippers"; say praying, services, the people in the room (Daniel's standing writing rule).
14. Course tone rules apply on top: sentence case, plain verbs, no exclamation points (so his "radical socialism!" register stays out), same words for a 12-year-old and a 60-year-old.
15. Use "we" and "us" for the community; address the learner as "you" only for an invitation.
16. **One teaching per blessing**, even for pairs said together (Daniel, 2026-10-24).
18. **Explaining a CRC service choice in the teaching is welcome** (Daniel chose to add the evening-haftarah sentence, 2026-10-24). C38's "leave it to the rabbi live" is weaker for the course than first read.
17. **Book-neutral.** Students hold the Beit Mitzvah book; never say "the Shabbat Morning book" or "the text printed here". Say "some siddurim", "many Reform siddurim".

## Tension to watch

- C38 says live framing belongs to the rabbi and the book stays structural. The course is a learning surface, not the prayer book, so it can carry his meaning framing; keep it short enough to leave room for live teaching. [Inferred]
