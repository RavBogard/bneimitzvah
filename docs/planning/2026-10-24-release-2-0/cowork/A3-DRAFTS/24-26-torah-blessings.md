# Torah blessings, before and after (BM p. 22; Shabbat Morning fol. 71–72)

History: draft 1 (one teaching for the pair) approved 2026-10-24; Daniel then ruled "One per blessing". Split into 24 and 26 below. Added sentences are marked NEW and need approval.

## 24. Blessing before the Torah reading, draft 2

An aliyah, a "going up" to the Torah, opens with a blessing. The one called up repeats Bar'chu, the same call that opened the service, now a doorway into the words themselves, and the congregation answers. The blessing then speaks of being chosen; we hear that choosing as a calling rather than a ranking, a responsibility to these words rather than a claim over anyone else. NEW: Being called up by name is one of the oldest honors in the service: before anyone reads, a person is asked to stand with the scroll and take responsibility for what is about to be heard.

Sources: approved draft 1; NEW sentence from rosh-hashanah/torah-service-rh.typ:435 (house footnote).

## 26. Blessing after the Torah reading, draft 2

After the reading comes a second blessing, and its verb is in the present tense: *noten*, the one who gives the Torah, not gave. The tradition insists that the giving was not a single event at Sinai but happens each time a community reads these words aloud. To say this blessing is to stand in that line, one more person to whom the Torah is being given, now. NEW: It also gives thanks for "a Torah of truth" and for "eternal life planted within us": the words do not stay on the scroll; they take root in the people who read them.

Sources: approved draft 1; NEW sentence: the blessing's own words (*torat emet, v'chayei olam nata b'tocheinu*), interpretation Claude's.
