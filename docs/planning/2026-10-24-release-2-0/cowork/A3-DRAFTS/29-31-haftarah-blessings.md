## 29. Blessing before the haftarah, draft 1

The haftarah, a "concluding" reading, is a selection from the prophets, most weeks chosen to echo something in the Torah portion. Before it, the reader blesses the prophets themselves, *n'vi'ei ha'emet v'hatzedek*, "prophets of truth and justice." That pairing is the heart of prophecy as Jews have kept it: the prophets stood before kings and crowds and named the gap between the world as it is and the world we are obliged to create. The blessing uses the language of choosing again, as the Torah blessing does, and we hear it the same way, as a calling. To chant the haftarah is to lend your voice, for a few minutes, to people who insisted on justice when it was unwelcome.

## 31. Blessings after the haftarah, draft 1

After the haftarah come blessings that move outward in rings: the prophet's words are true, so may the promises they carry be kept; then hopes for Zion and for redemption; and finally thanks for Shabbat itself. The hope for a descendant of David is old royal language. We can hear it as the tradition's refusal to let an unkept promise die, an insistence on holding an image of a redeemed world even, and perhaps especially, when it is hard to believe it can come. The blessings end where we are standing, in the holiness of this Shabbat, a taste of that world now.
