# CRC Torah usability review

November 20, 2026 · Public beta and main revision `fe15eb65`

CRC Torah makes some unusually difficult tasks work well. A student can go straight to their next assigned verse, hear a human chant it, and return to a particular word. A tutor can open the same passage during a lesson. The most consequential problems occur as people move between tasks. A recording covers the list it came from, a saved-reading address asks its recipient to sign in, and copying an invitation changes which link the family can use.

**Recommendation.** Keep the Hebrew reading surface and its visual identity. First fix the mobile recording player and the staff actions that silently replace a link or discard work. Give learners a clear way to share a customized passage and resume a course after sign-in. The evidence supports these targeted changes without a visual redesign.

## What the review covered

The source baseline was the November 20 remote main revision. The working checkout is a coordination branch 759 commits behind it. All 50 Next.js page modules were inventoried, including redirects, dynamic routes and the development gallery. Representative journeys were exercised in the public beta and in a clean local production build with synthetic sessions. The public beta and repository snapshot are identified separately wherever that distinction matters. [Source baseline](https://github.com/RavBogard/bneimitzvah/blob/fe15eb655b5cefa65a708dff8ef0c8467786759c/README.md) · [Live beta](https://beitmitzvah.centralreform.org)

Daniel’s working instructions identify **Annotated score** as the criterion. Current main documents **Song session** as the subsequent visual treatment. This review evaluates the actual blue-and-marigold interface against the shared functional priorities of legible Hebrew, restrained emphasis and nearby practice controls. [Current design guide](https://github.com/RavBogard/bneimitzvah/blob/fe15eb655b5cefa65a708dff8ef0c8467786759c/DESIGN.md#L174) · [Decision history](https://github.com/RavBogard/bneimitzvah/blob/fe15eb655b5cefa65a708dff8ef0c8467786759c/docs/planning/RULINGS.md#L29)

## Priority findings

**P1** indicates significant difficulty, a misleading action with consequences, or work that can be lost. **P2** indicates repeatable friction with a practical route around it. Severity reflects the affected task and the evidence. No participant task-success rate was measured.

### 1. P1 · The recording player can cover almost the entire phone

**Observed live.** On `/recordings`, choose the first Shermet recording from the unfiltered list. The sticky player expands upward. Keyboard focus stays on the original Listen button underneath it. Pause stops the sound but leaves the panel in place. The panel offers no collapse or close control, and Escape left it open. [Live library](https://beitmitzvah.centralreform.org/recordings) · [Player layout](https://github.com/RavBogard/bneimitzvah/blob/fe15eb655b5cefa65a708dff8ef0c8467786759c/src/components/shermet-library/library.module.css#L12)

| Viewport | Player height for the first result | Space above it |
|---|---:|---:|
| 320 × 568 | 557 px | 11 px |
| 390 × 844 | 473 px | 371 px |
| 844 × 390, landscape | 321 px | 69 px |

**Change and verify.** Keep title, play/pause and collapse in a compact player. Put speed, volume and seek in its expanded state. Selecting a recording, changing speed, closing the controls and selecting the next recording should all work at these sizes using touch and keyboard. The focused element must remain visible.

### 2. P1 · The saved-reading handoff falls short of the stated sharing goal

**Observed in the local build and source.** My Readings opens an owner address of the form `/read?saved=…`. Opening that shape of address signed out produces “Sign in to open this reading.” Public weekly and verse-reference addresses work, but the saved view exposes no equivalent handoff for its customized selection and date. The working product brief explicitly calls for an openly viewable customized reading. This matters when a tutor sends a passage before a Zoom lesson or an adult shares their preparation. [Working product brief](../../../PRODUCT.md) · [Shelf address](https://github.com/RavBogard/bneimitzvah/blob/fe15eb655b5cefa65a708dff8ef0c8467786759c/src/components/portal/mine/own-readings.tsx#L13) · [Saved-reading states](https://github.com/RavBogard/bneimitzvah/blob/fe15eb655b5cefa65a708dff8ef0c8467786759c/src/components/portal/saved/saved-reading.tsx#L31)

**Change and verify.** Add an explicit **Share passage** action alongside the saved reading. In a fresh signed-out browser, its link should open the chosen verses and date with normal practice tools. The owner’s name, notes, marks, student details and edit controls should stay private. Decide whether subsequent edits update the shared view or require a new link before specifying the implementation.

### 3. P1 · “Copy link” can invalidate the invitation a family already has

**Source-confirmed.** In Sent invitations, **Copy link** immediately requests a replacement link. The emailed link stops working. When copying succeeds the ordinary receipt says only “Link copied.” A staff member checking an invitation or pasting it into a note can unknowingly strand its recipient. The family-honors workflow already uses the more accurate **Replace link…** wording. [Invitation action](https://github.com/RavBogard/bneimitzvah/blob/fe15eb655b5cefa65a708dff8ef0c8467786759c/src/components/portal/workspace/invitations/workspace.tsx#L72) · [Success receipt](https://github.com/RavBogard/bneimitzvah/blob/fe15eb655b5cefa65a708dff8ef0c8467786759c/src/components/portal/workspace/invitations/workspace.tsx#L155) · [Honors precedent](https://github.com/RavBogard/bneimitzvah/blob/fe15eb655b5cefa65a708dff8ef0c8467786759c/src/components/portal/workspace/honors/honoree-controls.tsx)

**Change and verify.** Label the action **Replace and copy link**. Explain before activation that the previously sent link will stop working. If a replacement was created but clipboard access failed, distinguish those two outcomes and leave the new address available for manual copying. Preserve the existing invitation lifecycle.

### 4. P1 · Moving between staff pages silently discards congregation settings

**Reproduced with a synthetic admin on desktop and phone.** Type a draft in **Consent notice**, click **More**, then return with Back. The field is empty and there was no warning. The same page contains long source lists and service configuration. The roster and honors forms already protect unsaved work during navigation. [Settings form](https://github.com/RavBogard/bneimitzvah/blob/fe15eb655b5cefa65a708dff8ef0c8467786759c/src/components/portal/workspace/settings/workspace.tsx#L72) · [Roster departure handling](https://github.com/RavBogard/bneimitzvah/blob/fe15eb655b5cefa65a708dff8ef0c8467786759c/src/components/portal/workspace/roster.tsx)

**Change and verify.** Give a changed settings form the existing **Keep editing / Discard changes** behavior. Show its unsaved state near Save. Test a text edit and a checkbox change with workspace navigation, Back and a reload. A successful save should clear the warning.

### 5. P2 · A learner who signs in from a course loses their place

**Observed live and with synthetic sign-in state.** Both courses send their progress prompt to plain `/sign-in`. The page tells the visitor to use the account on their invitation, including an independent learner who came to keep course progress. A recognized account on this address lands at My Readings. The public reader already preserves a reading and selection across the same handoff. [Course prompt](https://github.com/RavBogard/bneimitzvah/blob/fe15eb655b5cefa65a708dff8ef0c8467786759c/src/components/learn/home.tsx#L109) · [Service course prompt](https://github.com/RavBogard/bneimitzvah/blob/fe15eb655b5cefa65a708dff8ef0c8467786759c/src/components/learn/service/home.tsx#L104) · [Sign-in copy](https://github.com/RavBogard/bneimitzvah/blob/fe15eb655b5cefa65a708dff8ef0c8467786759c/src/components/portal/entry.tsx#L247) · [Return destinations](https://github.com/RavBogard/bneimitzvah/blob/fe15eb655b5cefa65a708dff8ef0c8467786759c/src/lib/site/return-address.ts#L14)

**Change and verify.** Carry the specific course through sign-in using the existing checked return mechanism. Use invitation copy for invited visitors and goal-specific copy for course participants. Test both course homes, both sign-in methods and the existing reader Save flow.

### 6. P2 · The public reader is harder to start than the homepage suggests

**Observed live.** On a phone the opening reading presents Change/Save, Torah/Haftarah, Lein/Study, Start reading, aliyah navigation and a reading-tools dock. **Start reading** focuses the first verse, and in Lein requests playback when listening is available. The explanation of tapping a word or verse number appears farther down in the player. At 390 px, the week’s title “Vayetzei” also broke across two lines inside the word even though the page had no sideways overflow. Short Hebrew word targets were as small as 22 × 40 px. In the sampled passage only 14 of 204 Hebrew controls were sequential tab stops, so the keyboard task is smaller than the raw control count suggests. [Reader behavior](https://github.com/RavBogard/bneimitzvah/blob/fe15eb655b5cefa65a708dff8ef0c8467786759c/src/components/portal/public-reading/view.tsx#L590) · [Player instruction](https://github.com/RavBogard/bneimitzvah/blob/fe15eb655b5cefa65a708dff8ef0c8467786759c/src/components/portal/workspace/study/reading-player.tsx#L92) · [Title layout](https://github.com/RavBogard/bneimitzvah/blob/fe15eb655b5cefa65a708dff8ef0c8467786759c/src/components/portal/public-reading/public-reading.module.css#L13)

**Change and verify.** Put one short playback cue at the top of the passage, make the current verse visible after Start, and allow title actions to move to the next line before the name breaks. Keep the verse-number target, Hebrew marks, phrase colors and Zoom references intact. Before reorganizing the dock, have Daniel attempt “Hear this verse, repeat it, then return to Study” at 375–390 px and at enlarged text. The density judgment is a hypothesis for that walkthrough.

### 7. P2 · Finding a particular guide becomes expensive at real catalog scale

**Measured with a synthetic catalog.** The release state records 168 catalog keys. Supplying 168 synthetic single-guide entries to the actual admin component at the phone preset produced a **44,020 px** region with **336** buttons and disclosures and no search or filter field. The waiting queue usefully puts held work first. Finding a specific reading elsewhere still depends on scrolling, heading navigation or the browser’s Find command. [Release inventory](https://github.com/RavBogard/bneimitzvah/blob/fe15eb655b5cefa65a708dff8ef0c8467786759c/docs/planning/2026-10-15-kickoff/STATE.md) · [Guide list](https://github.com/RavBogard/bneimitzvah/blob/fe15eb655b5cefa65a708dff8ef0c8467786759c/src/components/portal/workspace/guide-admin/workspace.tsx#L185)

**Change and verify.** Keep the waiting queue. Add a reading-name search and state filter, and keep the exact version and reader-visible consequence next to Release or Hold. With a full catalog, a rabbi should be able to locate a named guide, inspect the version and return to the filtered list without losing their place.

## Smaller findings and strengths by task

- **Offline recovery.** The empty shelf correctly explains the 14-day window, but offers no action in its main content. Link an online visitor to an eligible reading or the existing install instructions. Tell someone already offline what to do on reconnect. The synthetic phone test successfully reopened and played an already-saved assigned passage. ## Heuristic assessment

The independent public-design assessment scored **31/40**. Incorporating the observed staff draft loss, invitation behavior and cross-account handoffs gives a **whole-product judgment of 28/40**. The two scopes answer different questions. Scores are expert judgments on a 0–4 scale, with 4 indicating excellent support.

| Heuristic | Score | Reason |
|---|---:|---|
| Visibility of system status | 3 | Named verses, lesson progress and save receipts are clear. The settings draft lacks a status. |
| Match with the user's language and task | 4 | Parashah, aliyah, verse and Tikkun are used in the actual context of study and practice. |
| User control and freedom | 2 | Library users cannot collapse the player. Staff can inadvertently lose a draft. |
| Consistency | 3 | Most pages share navigation, focus treatment and controls. Course and reader sign-in return differently. |
| Error prevention | 2 | Several editors handle conflicts well. Invitation replacement and settings departure are significant exceptions. |
| Recognition over recall | 3 | The next verse and named references help. A person looking for a guide must remember its place or use browser Find. |
| Flexibility and efficiency | 3 | Direct verse links, practice modes and keyboard navigation support experienced users. The guide catalog slows staff. |
| Visual hierarchy and restraint | 3 | The Hebrew passage is identifiable and readable. The phone header and expanded library player can dominate it or its surrounding task. |
| Error recovery | 3 | Form conflicts, offline status and retries are usually explicit. Some paths leave the next useful action elsewhere. |
| Help and documentation | 2 | Chart, credits, install help and lessons exist. Reading gestures and account expectations need explanation where people encounter them. |

## Verification and next walkthrough

- **Behavior.** Across focused local runs, 72 distinct test-and-browser combinations passed, including six combinations from review-specific probes. Device-specific skips were exercised on the intended device class where applicable. Two early attempts at the course-return probe used an unsuitable session fixture and then waited through an immediate redirect. The corrected probe passed with a recognized synthetic account. No live account, invitation, email or family record was used.
- **Accessibility.** axe-core 4.14.0 checked 12 public route/theme combinations at 390 px. Eleven produced no violations in the configured rules. Unsubscribe produced one best-practice heading finding. Keyboard inspection exposed the recording player covering focus, which the scan of the initial library state missed. Dedicated assistive-technology, physical-device and learner usability sessions remain unverified.
- **Design consistency.** Independent Impeccable detection on Home, Read and Learn produced 40 signals: 31 type-size, seven radius, one color and one font-documentation finding. The Hebrew face and modal scrim have clear purposes. These signals have lower priority than observed task failures. The scan covered selected public components, not every stylesheet.
- **Scope limits.** Live checks used anonymous Chromium sessions. Private paths used synthetic browser scenarios and source review. Physical printing, production sign-in delivery, live staff integrations, audio pedagogy and completion by a real student/tutor pair require separate evidence. The review made no application or deployment changes.
- **Acceptance order.** First, browse two recordings on a small phone and with keyboard only. Next, share a customized passage into a fresh session, replace an invitation knowingly, and leave a dirty settings form. Then resume each course after sign-in, find a named guide in a full catalog and recover from an empty offline shelf. For the core learning task, have Daniel open a Torah verse and a haftarah verse, hear, repeat, switch to Study and return to the same verse. Use those observations to decide whether the dock itself needs to change.
