# CRC Torah feature gap analysis

November 20, 2026 · Public beta · Release examined `1c9e2eff`

CRC Torah already lets someone find a passage, study it, hear a human chant it, rehearse with progressively less help, print it and share it. A tutor can assign exact verses, divide aliyot, model spoken Hebrew privately and assess progress. The biggest gaps arise when a learner moves between those capabilities. A child practicing through a parent cannot take their assignment into the full Learn experience. A student can complete a lesson without it counting as a practice day. A tutor can save an assignment without knowing which words the learner will be able to hear in the preferred voice. [Product decisions][rulings] · [Release state][state] · [Tutor and learner release][tutor]

**My priority before a real-family pilot** is to resolve the child-without-email path for the intended cohort, enforce the existing practice-history policy, and have Daniel walk one representative assignment from tutor setup through independent practice. For the next teaching improvement, show recording availability when the tutor chooses the verses. Reaching 100% catalog coverage would still leave the account and practice-history problems.

## What people can do today

| Person and task | Available in the reviewed release | Remaining limit |
|---|---|---|
| Visitor or adult preparing a reading | Find a weekly, holiday or custom passage. Switch between Lein and Study, Torah and haftarah. Use Hebrew word cards, grammar, stress, comparison, commentary, Tikkun and print. Sign in to keep a reading. | The dedicated Run-through uses an assigned student reading. An adult who saves next Friday’s reading has a reader and preparation tools, but their saved passage does not become the target of that run. [Word tools][words] · [Run-through][run] |
| Owner sharing a reading | Share an openly viewable snapshot of the exact verses and optional preparation date. The recipient can study or print independently. | Edits require sharing again. That is the approved behavior. A shared reading and a private honoree link serve different audiences and should stay distinct. [Sharing walkthrough][usability] · [Honoree controls][honors] |
| Assigned student | Open the next unfinished verse, listen beside the Hebrew, loop, change speed, mark “Got it,” return to difficult verses, and rehearse an assigned Torah aliyah from start to finish. | Full-verse audio and timed word practice have different coverage. A course-only practice day is also easy for the family to miss. [Playback release][aliyah] · [Recording coverage][shermet] · [Today card][today] |
| Learner developing chant skills | Hear and imitate a model, attempt a verse without it, compare, repair a difficult passage, fade from colors to marks to Tikkun, and review. Self-checks and tutor assessments each describe something different from a demonstrated learning outcome. [Preparation tests][prep] · [Readiness review][readiness] |

## Gaps and acceptance checks

The order reflects who loses access, how often the problem can occur and whether the product already promises the behavior. A proposed feature below is a product recommendation unless an existing decision is identified.

| Priority | Gap and consequence | What would close it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Family access | **Children without an individual email get a narrower learning path.** The invitation service rejects a parent’s mailbox for a student identity. Delegated Practice lasts 60 minutes. Learn resolves the assignment through the student’s own role, so a child using a parent’s sign-in cannot rehearse their own reading in the dedicated Run-through or use its personal verse review. The affected cohort size is unknown. [Invitation rules][invite] · [Own-reading lookup][own] · [Run-through][run] | Establish whether the pilot includes such a child. If it does, demonstrate with a synthetic two-child household that the selected child can resume the right assignment, practice it and retain the right progress after returning. Verify switching children, expiry and revocation before changing access. |
| 1 · Data lifecycle | **Practice history has an expiry policy without a scheduled cleanup.** Detailed activity is meant to expire after 90 days and retained dates one year after the ceremony. Cleanup code exists. The deployed cron configuration schedules other work, and the release state identifies no runner for retention. Filtering what appears on screen does not establish deletion. [Retention decision][rulings] · [Maintenance code][retention] · [Cron configuration][cron] | Connect a bounded recurring job or formally change the policy. Demonstrate cleanup of overdue synthetic detail and dates, preservation of current records, and completion after an interrupted run. |
| 1 · Teaching | **The tutor discovers recording limits after choosing the assignment.** Shermet covers 7,315 of 7,475 CRC baseline verses in full. Within that same baseline, 4,711 have her qualified word timing. Another credited recording may work where hers does not. The editor previews references, dates and divisions without showing this distinction. [Source inventory][shermet] · [Player fallback][fallback] · [Reading editor][editor] | Show the tutor which selected verses support word practice in the preferred voice, whole-verse practice, another credited source, or text only. Check a mixed Torah/haftarah assignment against the actual player. Keep every valid text assignable. |
| 2 · Practice and feedback | **“Days you practiced” counts a narrower activity than families may infer.** The calendar receives visits from the assigned workspace. Learn lessons, verse review, Run-through and service-course work have their own progress. A learner who uses only a course that day can see an empty dot. [Today card][today] · [Calendar writes][calendar] · [Student review][student-review] | Choose what the measure means. Either label it consistently as days in assigned Practice wherever a family or tutor sees it, or count agreed learning activities once per student per day. Check a course-only day, a combined day and parent-delegated practice. |
| 2 · Service preparation | **A learner has to reconcile several descriptions of the part they lead.** The live service order, assignment directions, past work and course completion each have a legitimate purpose. Moving from “I lead this blessing” to the right lesson, the right service version and the tutor’s feedback still crosses views. [Service workspace][service] · [Course home][course] · [Lesson recordings][lesson-audio] | For one morning part and one evening or Havdalah part, start at the assignment and reach the matching lesson, available recording and feedback. Return to the same part. Keep a removed or replaced part visibly distinct from current work. |
| 2 · Office setup | **Getting one student ready remains a multi-screen job.** Adding the person, verifying their reading, linking tutor and family, and preparing an invitation use different screens. The November walkthrough counted 12–15 taps in that version. Later invitation and access improvements removed friction, but the current release still lists a single setup flow as outstanding. [Prior walkthrough][old-review] · [Current release state][state] | From one setup checklist for a synthetic child or adult, finish each required action, leave and resume, and see exactly what remains. Unknown service slots stay unresolved. Invitation sending remains an explicit action. |
| 2 · Independent adult practice | **A dated saved passage lacks the assigned learner’s dedicated rehearsal.** This matters to an adult preparing one aliyah with no ceremony or tutor. They can study and hear it, but Run-through resolves a roster assignment. [Sharing walkthrough][usability] · [Run-through][run] | Test demand with Daniel’s weekly-preparation use case. A bounded extension would let the owner rehearse just the saved verses with fading cues and private retry marks. Shared-link visitors would continue to see only public passage material. |
| 2 · Content quality | **All 168 guide keys have base content; editorial review is unfinished.** The project specifically flags six sensitive guides for Daniel. Generated guides may appear with an unreviewed label. Portion summaries have a different approval rule. Treat the two publication states separately. [Release state][state] · [Editorial rulings][rulings] | Review the six named guides first or explicitly keep each held or labeled under the existing policy. Check the reader and print output for the correct version and credit after a hold, edit and release. |
| 2 · Integration correctness | **An emptied ceremony can leave names on the external music card.** The sender skips an empty ceremony and the receiver has no clear operation. The release record also leaves the production enablement flag unconfirmed. This is a conditional stale-data risk. [Sender and receiver contract][music] · [Release state][state] | Establish whether production sending is enabled. Agree on clearing semantics with the music receiver, then verify with synthetic records that moving the last student clears the old card and a delayed update cannot restore it. |
| 3 · Lesson usability | **Shared teaching and private editing still need predictable entry and exit.** Daniel identified a lesson sidebar that should begin collapsed and requested Close in the upper right across Practice and editing. Work was active in a separate checkout at the snapshot. [Walkthrough decisions][walk] · [Practice release][tutor] | Finish and verify that work on a narrow phone and a screen-shared desktop. Closing should restore the same passage and focus. Saved family-visible lesson summaries should stay reachable. |
| 3 · Personal continuity | **The latest lesson summary is visible, but specific feedback can be elsewhere.** Verse notes, marks and service feedback live beside their respective tasks. A student returning days later has no single route from the latest summary to every changed item. Course sign-in similarly returns to the course home in supported flows, and following an emailed link has a different landing. [Today card][today] · [Student review][student-review] · [Sign-in walkthrough][usability] | Observe whether Daniel can find a changed verse and service comment from the learner’s starting screen. If the handoff fails, link the existing items in place. Preserve the note types. Test resuming a particular lesson after sign-in before promising exact-step return. |

## What the recording numbers mean for a lesson

| Shermet coverage | Full-verse recordings | Qualified word timing |
|---|---:|---:|
| Within the 7,475-verse CRC baseline | 7,315 (97.9%) | 4,711 (63.0%) |
| Across ordinary verses mapped by the release, including custom-reading references | 7,434 | 4,786 |
| Within the 110 course references | 110 | 72 |

The course has a full model for every reference, but **38 course references lack qualified Shermet word timing**. A learner can hear the whole verse and then encounter a different voice or less granular practice when selecting a word. The counts describe Shermet alone. PocketTorah has separate timing, and the 110 Karaite and 300 Finkel selections are additional full recordings. Their catalog counts cannot be added to compute unique timed verses. Prioritize a verified report for an actual assignment, then improve timing where the teaching impact is highest. [Shermet inventory][shermet] · [Playback source selection][fallback] · [Additional recordings release][voices]

Speed and singing range also serve different needs. The release adjusts speed with pitch preservation. I found no learner control to transpose a human recording into a more comfortable key. Independent pitch adjustment was a desirable enhancement conditional on accurate timing. Give it lower priority than showing which existing recordings a learner can use. [Playback tempo][tempo] · [Source plan][shermet]

For independent rehearsal, the public **Prepare a passage** exercise already supports joining adjacent verses, trying the section without a recording, restarting at a familiar verse and returning by link. Its return link explicitly reopens the passage without saving the practice step. The useful extension for an adult is continuation on their own saved reading, including the verses they wanted to revisit. Building another silent-rehearsal exercise would duplicate an existing capability. [Public passage exercise][passage] · [Assigned Run-through][run]

## Evidence still needed

- **Learning outcome.** The browser tests exercise hearing a model, a silent attempt, comparison and repair. The remaining question is whether someone can prepare a passage, chant it with less help, recover after a mistake and repeat the process on another suitable passage several days later. Have Daniel walk that sequence first. A learner/tutor observation belongs to the pilot when he authorizes it. [Curriculum tests][prep] · [Learning evaluation][readiness]
- **A complete family journey.** Existing releases provide synthetic coverage of private workflows. The recorded pilot status gives no completed real-family journey from invitation to independent practice and the next tutor lesson. Observe where someone needs a workaround, including with a parent’s device. [Pilot ruling][rulings] · [Usability evidence][ux-review]
- **Assistive technology and print.** Public automated accessibility checks, enlarged-text, keyboard and phone-profile evidence cover important cases. Read a mixed Hebrew/English verse and find playback controls with a screen reader, verify a word highlight survives magnification, and inspect a physical practice sheet for readable marks, credits and verse boundaries. [Accessibility baseline][ux-review] · [Release walkthrough][usability]
- **Smaller operational items.** The planned `torah.centralreform.org` cutover depends on firewall and DNS. The current staff handoff also flags re-designating a removed and re-granted tutor as an approver, and updating attribution after replacing the contents of an imported guide. Each has a specific owner or condition and can be tracked independently of the learning pilot. [Release state][state]

## Decisions that keep the backlog honest

The November 16 review identified missing service-course links, a missing next action for students, unused tutor assessments and absent web controls for guides. Subsequent releases linked the service course and Run-through, added the next unfinished verse, made tutor Solid assessments inform staff status, added per-aliyah progress and “Since your last lesson,” and shipped guide filtering, release and hold. The floating verse player was replaced with inline controls. Whole-aliyah reference playback reached main and its deployment during this analysis. [Current state][state] · [Usability release][usability] · [Whole-aliyah change][aliyah]

Daniel explicitly declined cross-student practice dashboards, a Claude student-status tool, a private tutor-to-rabbi line, weekly verse assignments, bulk invitations and dated family timelines. Student recording/submission, student translation drafts, CSV upload and the roster Sheet were removed. Private tutor-spoken models are a separate shipped feature. Notifications remain parked. Generated voices retain their future per-system G1/G2/G3 gates. D’var drafting, mitzvah projects and general family logistics are outside this study product. None belongs in the pilot gap count. [Decisions in force][rulings] · [Tutor-spoken models][tutor]

## Evidence and scope

The active checkout is an older coordination branch. I compared its mission, amendments and handoff with remote main, current decisions, source, tests and dated release reports. I pinned the analysis to `1c9e2eff` after confirming that main advanced during the review and that the deployment identifier on the canonical homepage matched the successful Vercel status for that commit. Seven anonymous public routes responded successfully. At phone width I inspected Home, Read, the trope chart, the service course and Recordings, and followed the Zakef chart entry into its lesson. Private-role statements use source, synthetic test evidence and prior release walkthroughs. I did not exercise a live family account, listen to the full catalog, rerun CI, or measure learner outcomes. Priority and acceptance checks are my product judgments.
