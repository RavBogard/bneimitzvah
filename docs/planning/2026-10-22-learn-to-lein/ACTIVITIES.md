# Learn to lein: drills, little tests and activities

2026-10-23. Written for Daniel and for Claude Code. Companion to COURSE.md (where each activity sits) and lessons.json (which activities each lesson calls, with parameters).

## How to read this

Every activity below is built from data that already exists in the repo: the trope engine's phrase families per word (`verseUnits` / `colorStudyVerse`), the PocketTorah word timings (any word range of any word-ready verse can be cut and played), the melody cells, and this week's reading from the public API. Where something is missing, the entry says so. "Phone fit" means 360 px wide, one thumb, no horizontal scroll. "Signed out" means it works with no account; progress for signed-out learners lives only in the browser.

Two rules that run through every activity, from the research (REPORT.md, conclusions 3, 5 and 8):

- The learner answers before the answer is shown. Listening-only screens come first in a lesson, and then every screen is a small retrieval attempt.
- After a wrong answer the app plays the correct phrase, then the chosen one, then re-asks the item a few items later. The feedback is about the melody, never about the learner. There is no score on screen.

## The v1 set (ships in 1.5)

### 1. Hear and name  (`hear-and-name`)

- What the learner does: a phrase plays (cut from a PocketTorah verse by word timings). The learner taps one of two to four family names. Each choice is a chip with the family's name, colour and contour mark. Tap the phrase to hear it again, as often as you like.
- Teaches: the core skill, sound to family. In the early lessons the choices are just the two or three families learned so far; later the choices include the family's usual confusions (zakef katan against revia, tevir against etnachta, kadma v'azla against pazer).
- Feedback: right, the chip settles into its colour and the phrase is shown in its verse with the phrase coloured. Wrong, the correct chip settles, the chosen family's phrase plays once for contrast, and the item comes back three to five items later. No score. At the end of a run, missed items return until each has been answered right once.
- Data: word timings; families per word; a pool of candidate phrases per family (generated by rule: word-ready verses, phrase of 1 to 5 words, not the last verse of an aliyah or haftarah unless the family is sof pasuk or sof haftarah at the end of a reading, not the special-melody passages). All exists today.
- Phone fit: the phrase's words at Hebrew size Standard, then a column of 2 to 4 chips, each 56 px tall. One thumb.
- Signed out: yes. Signed in: each answer updates the family's review card.
- Build: S. One component; the item generator is a filter over the engine's output.
- Why it's fun, not cheesy: every item is a real verse, so recognising the tune is recognising Torah; the colour arriving on the right chip is the whole reward.

### 2. Same or different  (`same-or-different`)

- What the learner does: two short phrases play one after the other (a short gap between them). The learner taps "Same tune" or "Different tune". The two phrases are always from different verses, so "same" never means the same words.
- Teaches: hearing the melodic shape at all, before naming it. This is the first drill in the first two lessons and the drill that comes back when a learner misses a lot in hear-and-name.
- Feedback: the answer appears with both families named and coloured; wrong items replay both phrases back to back and return later. No score.
- Data: same pools as hear-and-name. Pairs are built by rule: half same-family, half different, with "different" drawn from the families learned so far and, from unit 2 on, biased to the confusable neighbour.
- Phone fit: two large play tiles stacked, two buttons below. Fits with room.
- Signed out: yes.
- Build: S.
- Why it's fun, not cheesy: it is a listening game with no reading at all, and adults who "can't read music" win at it in the first minute.

### 3. Tap the phrase you heard  (`tap-the-phrase`)

- What the learner does: a whole verse is shown, uncoloured, with its phrase boundaries marked only by small gaps. One phrase from it plays. The learner taps anywhere inside the phrase they heard.
- Teaches: linking the sound to the marks on real words, which is what reading from a text needs. It is also the first time the learner sees a phrase inside its verse rather than alone.
- Feedback: the tapped phrase takes its family colour and legend chip; if wrong, the right phrase colours instead and both play in turn. Items with a wrong answer return later.
- Data: families and phrase indices per word (`colorStudyVerse` gives `phrase` per word); word timings for the cut. All exists today. The verse is one with 3 to 6 phrases.
- Phone fit: the verse wraps at Hebrew Standard, tap targets are whole phrases (44 px minimum, they always are at this size). The play tile sits above the verse so the thumb never covers the text.
- Signed out: yes.
- Build: M (tap targets over a wrapped RTL verse; the engine already gives the word ranges).
- Why it's fun, not cheesy: the verse colours in as you get it right, which is the reading page's own look arriving one phrase at a time.

### 4. Where does the phrase end  (`where-does-it-end`)

- What the learner does: a verse is shown without colours. A phrase starts playing from a given word; the audio stops at the end of the phrase. The learner taps the word they think the phrase ended on. Variant for later lessons: no audio at all, read the marks and tap the word that carries the closing accent.
- Teaches: the one piece of grammar that matters most to leyning, which is where to pause. Tipcha, pashta, yetiv and zarka lead into the phrase after them; conjunctives never end one. Teachers say the commonest beginner error is pausing on a conjunctive or running through a disjunctive (REPORT.md, A1).
- Feedback: the right word gets the family colour and the phrase is bracketed; a wrong tap shows the word's own mark name ("that's a munach, which joins") and plays the phrase again. Returns later.
- Data: per-word accent and family (exists), word timings (exists).
- Phone fit: words are the tap targets; at Hebrew Standard every word is at least 44 px tall.
- Signed out: yes.
- Build: M.
- Why it's fun, not cheesy: it feels like following a line of music with your finger, and it is the first drill where the learner is reading, not only listening.

### 5. Build the phrase  (`build-the-phrase`)

- What the learner does: a phrase plays. Below it are the marks it uses, drawn on a sample letter (as the trope chart draws them), shuffled, plus one or two that do not belong. The learner taps them into order. For a phrase like mercha tipcha silluq that is three taps.
- Teaches: which marks make the family and in what order; the conjunctives that belong to each phrase (munach before etnachta, mahpach and pashta before zakef, darga before tevir, kadma before azla).
- Feedback: as each mark is placed it snaps into the row and the row plays itself so far, from the recording, word by word; a wrong mark shakes once and returns to the tray. At the end the whole phrase plays with the marks shown on the real words.
- Data: per-word accent names in order (exists: `verseUnits` gives `accents` and `trope.accent`); the chart's sample-letter rendering (exists in `chart-catalog`). Phrases are chosen by rule so that each mark sits on its own word (no two accents on one word) and the phrase has 2 to 4 words.
- Phone fit: a row of 2 to 4 slots, a tray of 3 to 6 mark tiles at 56 px.
- Signed out: yes.
- Build: M.
- Why it's fun, not cheesy: it is the only drill that is hands-on, and hearing the row play itself as you build it is quietly satisfying.

### 6. In this week's reading  (`this-week`)

- What the learner does: every family lesson ends with a short list of places where that family occurs in this Shabbat's Torah and haftarah (from `/api/public/reading?date=`). Each row shows the phrase in its verse, coloured, with a play tile. A one-line prompt: "Find three more." The learner scrolls the reading in the course's own small reader and taps phrases of that family; the app confirms each and plays it.
- Teaches: transfer. The family stops being a flashcard and becomes something you meet in the text people will read on Saturday.
- Feedback: a correct tap colours the phrase and plays it; a wrong tap names the phrase's real family and plays it. No count displayed beyond "3 of 3 found". When the week's reading has no word-ready verses (the 18 special haftarot), the Torah side still works and the haftarah side says "No recording for this haftarah yet."
- Data: this week's ranges (exists), families per word (exists), timings (exists for Torah 99.4%, haftarot about 72%).
- Phone fit: a list of rows at Hebrew Standard; the reader is the existing continuous passage at a smaller step.
- Signed out: yes.
- Build: M (reuses the reading page's components).
- Why it's fun, not cheesy: the text is this week's, so it changes on its own every Saturday night, and what you learned on Tuesday is on the bimah on Shabbat.

### 7. Review  (`review`)

- What the learner does: when any family card is due, the course home shows one calm line ("Review: 5 phrases, about 2 minutes"). The review is a mixed run of hear-and-name, same-or-different, tap-the-phrase and where-does-it-end items over the due families, 8 to 12 items, missed items re-asked at the end.
- Teaches: keeping it. Spaced retrieval is the best-supported finding in the whole report (REPORT.md, A2).
- Feedback: as in the drills. At the end, one line: "Done. These come back on Thursday." (the day comes from the schedule; no notification is sent).
- Data: a per-learner schedule. Does not exist yet. COURSE.md specifies it (Leitner boxes; same session, 1, 3, 7, 16, 35 days). Signed out, the schedule lives in localStorage; signed in, it lives with the learner's progress.
- Phone fit: same screens as the drills.
- Signed out: yes (browser only). Signed in: synced.
- Build: M (the scheduler is about 80 lines; the screens are reused).
- Why it's fun, not cheesy: it is short, it never nags, and the learner sees what came back.

### 8. Your first verse  (`first-verse`)

- What the learner does: the last lesson of each melody path. The app picks a verse from this week's reading (shortest word-ready verse, preferring one that uses only common families; the learner can pick another). The verse appears whole, coloured. Step 1: hear each phrase in turn (tap to replay). Step 2: hear the verse whole. Step 3: chant along with the recording at Speed 0.75, then at 1. Step 4: "Open in Lein", which opens the reading page on that verse with Repeat and Speed already there.
- Teaches: the goal of the course. Nothing new; everything learned, in one place.
- Feedback: none is scored. Finishing step 3 is the course's one celebration moment (COURSE.md says what it looks like).
- Data: this week's reading, families, timings, the Lein page. All exists. The only new piece is a deep link into Lein with a verse selected.
- Phone fit: the verse at Hebrew Large, one play tile, one Next button. The lifted-line close-up is available but not required.
- Signed out: yes. Signed in: "Your first verse" is marked done on the learner's progress.
- Build: M.
- Why it's fun, not cheesy: it is the moment the whole thing was for, and the app gets out of the way.

## Later

### 9. Same sign, different place  (`look-alikes`)  S, later but first

Two words appear, each carrying the same-looking sign in a different place (pashta on the last letter, kadma on the stressed syllable; mahpach inside the word, yetiv before the first letter; geresh and azla). The learner hears one and taps the word it belongs to, or reads only and names each. Teaches the three confusions every teacher lists (REPORT.md, A1). Needs per-word accents (exists). Cheap to build; it is "later" only because v1 is already eight.

### 10. Fill the gap  (`fill-the-gap`)  M

The verse plays with one phrase silent (the cut skips its word range). The learner reads the marks on the silent words and names the family, then hears it. Teaches reading marks into sound, the direction the course needs most at the end. Needs a two-range playback (the player can already cut any range; silence between two cuts is a small addition).

### 11. Odd one out  (`odd-one-out`)  S

Three phrases play; two share a family. Tap the odd one. Fine discrimination for the review queue once a learner is solid.

### 12. Which half of the verse  (`which-half`)  S

A phrase plays; the learner says whether it came before or after the etnachta. A quiet way to teach that the verse has two halves and that the tune tells you which you are in. Needs only phrase index per word (exists).

### 13. Quick ten  (`quick-ten`)  S

Ten hear-and-name items with no pauses between them and no timer. Calm by default; a timer would be a rule change and is not proposed.

### 14. Tap the stress  (`tap-the-stress`)  M

A word appears; the learner taps the syllable that carries the stress, then hears it. Teachers say wrong stress makes the whole motif wrong (REPORT.md, A1). The engine already computes syllables and stress (`verseUnits` gives `syllables` with the main accent). Shown without transliteration: the Hebrew word with syllable gaps, which is why it is M, not S.

### 15. Trope song, call and response  (`trope-song`)  M, needs a recording

The app sings a short run of trope names (zarka segol, munach revia, mahpach pashta munach zakef katon, and so on); the learner sings it back; the next run follows. Every teacher uses the song as a review mnemonic, not as lesson one. There is no human recording of the trope names sung on their melodies anywhere in the repo, and PocketTorah has none, so this needs a new recording. Whether to make one is in QUESTIONS-FOR-DANIEL.md. Until then the review queue does the song's job with real phrases.

### 16. Hands-free listening  (`listen-list`)  S

A play-all list of the lesson's examples, each announced by its family name in the UI (no synthetic voice), for the car ride. Needs only a playlist over the existing cuts.

### 17. Compare your chant  (`compare`)  M, Daniel decides

Hear the reference phrase, record yourself, see the two melody lines one above the other, nothing stored, nothing sent, no score. REPORT.md A4 covers feasibility, honesty and privacy. It is in QUESTIONS-FOR-DANIEL.md because it records a voice.

## Item generation rules used by lessons.json

- Pools: for a family F in melody M, the pool is every phrase of family F in a word-ready verse of table M, 1 to 5 words long, excluding the special-melody passages (Gen 1:5, 8, 13, 19, 23, 31; Ex 15:1–19; Num 33:11–49; Ex 20 and Deut 5; Gen 35:22) and excluding the last verse of a Torah aliyah recording and the last verse of a haftarah for the sof pasuk family (their endings differ). For the sof-haftarah family the pool is only the last verse of each haftarah recording.
- Distractors: by default, the families the learner has completed (one exception: the first lesson of each path contrasts sof pasuk with etnachta, which the intro lesson has already played as "the rest"), weighted 2 to 1 toward each family's confusable neighbours: sof pasuk with etnachta; zakef with revia and with zakef gadol; tevir with etnachta; kadma v'azla with pazer and with telisha ketana; segol with zakef; telisha ketana with kadma v'azla; pazer with telisha ketana; zakef gadol with zakef.
- Counts: a lesson's drills total 8 to 14 planned items. Missed items are re-asked until right once, so a lesson can run a little longer for a learner who is struggling; a lesson's drills may stop early once eight items in a row have been answered right with no miss.
- Verse length: prefer verses of 5 to 12 words for tap-the-phrase and where-does-it-end.
- Variety: never two items in a row from the same verse; spread across books.
