## Primary Torah starter

**Select Genesis 1:1 as the first authoring candidate.** It already appears in the introduction, etnachta and first-verse lessons, so this connects existing content rather than requiring a new asset hunt.

| Teaching use | Existing engine unit range | Intended task |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whole verse | 0–6 | Hear the complete task, later attempt and compare it |
| A, etnachta phrase | 0–2 | Model/echo its actual words; optional sign/name connection |
| B, ordinary ending phrase | 3–6 | Model/echo the ending and its lead-in |
| A→B bridge | A suitable range across units 2 and 3, with enough surrounding context | Teach the join, then return to 0–6; determine the usable span from actual phrasing, not an arbitrary two-word cut |

Ranges are zero-based inclusive engine units; use the existing text/timeline projection at runtime. No inferred millisecond timings. The bridge span is deliberately not frozen as an unreviewed musical cut.

Default novice flow can begin with one phrase and then teach the other; either way the listener hears each anchor before an optional contrast. An experienced reader can go straight to the whole attempt. Both parts of one verified continuous source are a useful way to reduce uncontrolled source variation; the catalogue alone does not establish singer identity or voice consistency.

Use this verse as a clearly labeled teaching example when the chosen weekly/shared reading is unsuitable for a first task. Keep a return link to that reading. Do not silently substitute it while calling it the learner's own reading.

## Before an example is used in teaching

1. Resolve the exact reference and melody through existing public/reference contracts. Confirm text/timing identity, usable word boundaries and ready audio; preserve attribution and licensing behavior.
2. Listen through the selected units and surrounding context as ordinary content QA. Check actual word alignment, intelligibility, phrase integrity and the named teaching target. Creator credit alone does not establish speaker identity.
3. Use a model appropriate to the selected tradition and explain legitimate variation; do not require copying the model's vocal range or timbre.
4. Check that early recognition alternatives were taught and are unambiguous at the stated level. Broad chart-family membership alone does not validate a sound-equivalence answer.
5. Exercise missing-audio/text-only behavior: retry or a clearly labeled suitable example, with the chosen reading still reachable. No unheard-model completion claim.

The current source identifies a fixed public PocketTorah attribution and strict reference/short-source parsers. Preserve them. This proposal needs no new speaker-identification service, new recordings, generated audio or widening of media metadata contracts.
