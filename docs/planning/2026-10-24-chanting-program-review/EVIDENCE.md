# Evidence behind the structural review

24 October 2026. Targeted research and source review, not an exhaustive systematic literature review. No claims about unpublished studies or a uniquely best cantillation method.

## Evidence classes

- **Current implementation:** source at main `4a99b7f` in the study checkout, plus the previously verified lesson/activity inventory. An independent read-only review traced support tools and their relationship to the curriculum.
- **Direct learner evidence:** Daniel's reported first same/different failure with different voices, lack of word-following in lessons and dissatisfaction with active-word fill. This is important usability evidence, not a sample sufficient to estimate efficacy for everyone.
- **Recorded intentions:** P4/N17/N18/N19, the service-course/Hebrew plans and current product rulings. Planned work is not counted as delivered capability.
- **General learning evidence/frameworks:** the sources below. Applications to Torah chanting are explicit instructional judgments.
- **Unverified outcome:** no demonstrated beginner-to-independent-chanting result for this application was supplied or observed. No fresh actual-clip audition, physical-device review or learner pilot occurred here.

## Primary sources and bounded implications

### Complex skill integration

[Four-Component Instructional Design, model authors' site](https://www.4cid.org/about/).

The framework connects learning tasks, explanatory support, procedural help and targeted practice. It is useful for asking how separate skills are integrated. This is a design framework; its use here does not validate the proposed chanting program or dictate every task sequence. The concrete architecture in this review is our domain-specific proposal.

### Transfer and preparation for new tasks

[National Research Council, How People Learn, Chapter 3: Learning and Transfer](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/9853/chapter/6).

The synthesis distinguishes performance on practiced material from transfer and examines the roles of understanding, prior knowledge, context and help. It supports examining new preparation tasks and the help required. It does not establish that family-recognition drills transfer to vocal performance, nor prescribe surprise sight-reading as an assessment.

### Feedback and self-regulation

[National Research Council, Education for Life and Work, Chapter 4](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/13398/chapter/6).

The chapter discusses feedback quality/timing and learning to regulate practice. Feedback that explains a difficulty has a different instructional role from simply flagging it. Our application is to teach a repeatable compare-and-repair process; the source does not specify an audio-scoring system or prove that novices can accurately assess their own chanting.

### Appropriate challenge and performance versus learning

[Bjork and Bjork, author manuscript on desirable difficulties](https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2021/01/RABjorkELBjorkJARMAC2020ForPostingSingleSpaced.pdf).

The authors distinguish productive challenges from difficulties a learner lacks the preparation to use, and distinguish immediate performance from retention/transfer. That cautions against using frustration alone as evidence of useful challenge, or easy immediate success as evidence of lasting learning. It does not prescribe removal of all retrieval practice. The proposed early support and later variation still need validation here.

### Practice quality in music

[Duke, Simmons and Cash, It's Not How Much; It's How, research abstract](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ824079).

This observational study examined 17 advanced pianists preparing a short passage for a next-day performance. Its findings make practice behavior and performance a useful subject for inquiry rather than simply counting time/repetitions. It is small, observational, instrumental and not a study of beginner cantillation. It supplies no chanting time guarantee or universal practice dosage.

### Direct cantillation teaching precedent

[Kol Kore, Cantillation teachers' guide](https://kol-kore.org/en/cantillation-trope-teachers-guide/).

The guide links listening, repeating, finding patterns in text and practicing readings. It shows a relevant practitioner model in which sound and actual text are connected. It is not a controlled effectiveness study and does not settle CRC's melody, exact teaching order or standards.

### General instruction and retrieval

[IES, Organizing Instruction and Study to Improve Student Learning](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1).

The guide supports combining worked examples and practice, connecting representations and spacing learning. General support for retrieval does not establish a need to make every screen a quiz, or validate the current exact card schedule and compulsory question sequence. Repeated listening with imitation and correction is also more than passive exposure.

## Audit of earlier rationale

The older `docs/superpowers/research/2026-10-22-learn-to-lein/REPORT.md` is useful background. It should be read with these corrections:

| Earlier conclusion | Appropriate confidence now |
| --- | --- |
| Every surveyed curriculum uses the same early family order | Source access included catalogues/reviews and incomplete book contents. Treat the order as a plausible instructional choice, not a demonstrated universal standard. |
| Retrieval effects imply every screen should ask a question | That exact interaction rule is an extrapolation; it is not established for this novice vocal task. |
| A specific modified Leitner schedule is enough | An engineering/design choice informed by spacing research; not a tested dosage for this program. |
| Short lesson duration and daily completion imply course completion in weeks | Unsupported as a learning promise. The difficulty, starting skills, correction needs and retention outcome matter. |
| Category recognition can precede production as a universal staircase | Recognizing a label and producing sound are different tasks. Their teaching relationship needs explicit design and evidence. |
| A variation-rich pool automatically creates useful transfer | Variation can help, but item suitability, instruction and the transfer outcome must be examined. |

Do not silently rewrite those historical research documents or treat all recommendations here as newly approved mission changes. This review supplies a higher-level evidence interpretation for coordination.

## What the research does not let us claim

No selected source demonstrates that this application works, guarantees that every beginner can become capable without additional help, defines a universally correct mastery threshold, or makes the whole proposed program empirically validated. The report is a reasoned structural assessment with a plan for obtaining the missing outcome evidence.
