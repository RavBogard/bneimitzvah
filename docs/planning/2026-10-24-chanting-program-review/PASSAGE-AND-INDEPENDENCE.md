# Teaching beyond the first verse

24 October 2026. Detailed design draft continuing [WORKED-FIRST-READING.md](WORKED-FIRST-READING.md). This fills the preparation, integration, recovery and transfer work that a family catalogue alone does not specify. Basic decoding is assumed; tasks and practice depth vary by existing ability.

The identifiers below are planning labels, not new app lesson IDs or schema. Deliver through existing reading/practice surfaces where useful rather than create a parallel player, progress engine or separate course application.

## B1. Prepare a manageable passage

**Teach:** preview the chosen text and hear its model; identify manageable parts and the first uncertainty. Start with an appropriately small passage, not an arbitrary universal verse count. Length, musical patterns, unfamiliar words and current ability all matter.

**Proposed learner copy:** “Choose a short section to prepare. Listen through, then pick one part to work on.” Let the person choose a reference-backed example when their desired passage lacks usable audio. Keep the desired reading reachable and label the alternative honestly.

**Practice:** apply words/model/echo/independent-attempt/repair to each part. An experienced reader can skip the introductory explanations and prepare directly. A person who knows signs but needs production help can use the models. An ear-first reader can use recording-led preparation without a naming gate.

**Check/repair:** distinguish “I need the word”, “I need the sound” and “I need to connect these parts”. Each opens relevant existing help and returns to the passage. Allow uncertainty rather than demand an unsupported accuracy judgment.

**Useful result:** the person has practiced preparing parts and can identify a next task. Opening every word tool or playing every clip is not proof of preparation.

## B2. Join verses into a connected reading

**Teach:** a verse boundary needs practice just as a phrase boundary can. Hear the final phrase of one verse and the beginning of the next in a coherent reference context. Explain the text location so the learner can find the join again.

**Copy:** “You’ve worked on the parts. Now practice how they connect.” Model the transition, rehearse it, then include both surrounding verses. Provide a local retry without replaying the entire passage every time.

**Check/repair:** if words are secure but the transition is uncertain, use the bridge recipe. If the new verse loses its opening, replay its beginning, then the bridge. Practice the actual context; do not assume a change in recording/source voice is a musical instruction.

**Useful result:** an independent attempt across the passage with a reference available after. No claim of fluency from successful separate clips. A text-based location cue supports continuity without testing whether the person can remember verse numbers.

## B3. Learn a repeatable practice method

**Teach:** choose a goal, make an attempt, identify the first useful repair, practice locally, rejoin, and select a return point. Demonstrate this sequence in a real passage rather than merely list player controls.

**Copy:** “Try the section once. Choose one thing to work on, then try the section again.” Offer word, tune, join and finding-place routes. A person can move between them as the difficulty changes.

**Practice:** keep this a short actionable cycle. More attempts are available, with no fixed count presented as proof of learning. If difficulty persists, change the unit/help rather than repeat an unexplained exercise indefinitely.

**Useful result:** the person can choose useful work and return it to the passage. Do not assess strategy solely by total time, number of clicks or completion of a practice-day marker. Existing progress/persistence stays within its authority; no new account requirement or notification is introduced.

## B4. Reduce assistance for the intended task

**Teach:** distinguish preparing with a model from chanting while a model leads. Begin an independent attempt without simultaneous audio, then use reference afterward. Explain what the person is trying to retrieve and keep a route back to specific support.

**Copy:** “Try without the recording. Replay the part you want to check afterward.” Existing color/mark/text views remain aids. When useful, invite a less-supported view; do not require every person to pass every view or remove all aids simultaneously.

**Check/repair:** if performance collapses without the leader, restore a short model and rehearse retrieval on a smaller unit, then rejoin. If a visual cue is the difficulty, restore that cue and practice the relevant text. Avoid treating playback dependence and sign knowledge as the same problem.

**Useful result:** a prepared attempt under the intended support conditions, plus a way to repair. Using resources during preparation is compatible with independence. No app-certified voice judgment or mandatory tutor gate follows.

## B5. Move toward unpointed reading where relevant

**Teach:** use a familiar prepared passage, compare pointed and unpointed forms, locate the same words, attempt a manageable span and restore support where necessary. Existing tikkun views are tools for this progression, not the teaching itself.

**Copy:** “Find the same words in the letters-only text. Try this familiar section, then open the pointed text wherever you need it.” Do not introduce unfamiliar text and remove its help at the same moment.

**Check/repair:** distinguish a word/reading-form uncertainty from forgetting the melody or losing place. Resolve the actual difficulty, then retry the unpointed span in context. A person whose intended task uses pointed text can focus on that task rather than face an irrelevant gate.

**Useful result:** a prepared reading under appropriate text conditions, not a universal claim of unpointed Hebrew fluency. Preserve existing qere/ketiv/read-form handling; this design does not override the renderer's text contract.

## B6. Retain place and recover

**Teach:** rehearse an ordinary stumble in already-prepared material. Model a calm return to a known textual/musical starting point and continuation. Specific public-reading correction etiquette remains congregational/domain content; do not invent a universal ritual instruction.

**Copy:** “If you lose your place, return to a part you know, find the next words, and continue.” Treat this as a practice strategy, with tradition-specific performance guidance supplied where applicable.

**Practice:** find a nearby known point, re-enter the phrase, connect to what follows and retry a continuous span. An optional competent listener can help identify a useful restart. Do not deliberately provoke confusion with mismatched audio or hidden text.

**Useful result:** a person can locate, restore and continue, rather than restart the entire reading after every error. Navigation buttons alone do not establish this skill. No public tally, leaderboard or permanent failure label.

## B7. Return after a delay and prepare a different passage

**Teach:** after earlier work, try a familiar span before replay, identify what faded and repair it. Then choose fresh appropriate material and apply the same preview/parts/repair/joins/independent-attempt process with normal resources.

**Copy:** “Try the part you prepared earlier. Revisit anything uncertain.” For fresh work: “Use the same preparation steps with this reading. Choose where to begin.” Do not prescribe a research-proven fixed interval or treat a surprise performance as preparation transfer.

**Check/repair:** observe what support is used and whether it helps; repeated use of the reference remains legitimate preparation. Recognition of a new family excerpt is a different outcome from preparing new text.

**Useful result:** retention, recovery of faded work and a reusable preparation process. If later outcome evaluation is authorized, compare these against the person's starting ability. Experienced readers may benefit from efficient targeted practice rather than introductory gains.

## Different starting abilities across these tasks

| Starting situation | Useful first work | Do not assume |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New chanter with basic decoding | First phrase/verse, then a small B1 passage with more modeling | Comfortable decoding supplies melody or a preparation method |
| Strong imitation or a memorized reading | B2/B3/B4 and a new B7 passage, with optional signs | One polished memorized performance supplies general preparation skill |
| Partial/rusty trope knowledge | Current passage, relevant sound refresher, B2/B4/B7 | A forgotten label means all ability is lost, or remembered labels prove production |
| Experienced preparation | Own reading, difficult joins, B5/B6 or special-context help | Everyone needs the same introductory sequence or repetition |

The same public tools and library support these starts. No fixed rank or mandatory placement exam. A public shared reading supplies content context, not its owner's private profile or progress.
