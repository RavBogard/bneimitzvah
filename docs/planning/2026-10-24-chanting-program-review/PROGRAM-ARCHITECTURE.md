# Proposed architecture of the chanting program

24 October 2026. Instructional proposal for coordination. No application implementation or replacement roadmap is authorized here. The [structural review](REVIEW.md) explains the evidence and judgment; the earlier [lesson map](../2026-10-24-learning-path-review/EXERCISE-AND-LESSON-MAP.md) retains the detailed activity inventory.

## One learner-facing spine, several supporting capabilities

The spine is preparation and chanting of real text. **Basic Hebrew phonetic decoding is assumed (Daniel's clarification).** Word-specific help, family sounds, mark patterns, phrase structure, meaning and review are supporting strands. They should be reachable when they help the current task, without becoming a catalogue of mandatory prerequisites.

[ENTRY-POINTS-AND-SUPPORT.md](ENTRY-POINTS-AND-SUPPORT.md) adds flexible entry for new chanters, ear-experienced readers, partial/rusty trope users and experienced readers. The task levels below describe capabilities, not a compulsory linear sequence for every person. Let learners start at useful work, bypass familiar instruction and return to needed support; musical imitation, sign knowledge and independence may be at different levels.

Keep the common public reading/learning experience. A signed-in owner can customize a reading for a day; an open shared link can supply the same reading context for someone else. Ownership determines editing/persistence, not access to basic teaching. Private progress and tutor information do not become public through the content link.

## A complete teaching loop

For a useful passage at an appropriate difficulty:

1. **Orient:** identify the text, intended attempt and reason for the next activity.
2. **Model:** hear and see a clear, accurate reference with a specific point of attention.
3. **Attempt with support:** echo, work on the words and try the phrase while the model/cues remain available.
4. **Check:** attend to one relevant feature and compare with a useful reference or competent feedback.
5. **Repair:** choose a targeted action, then use it in the phrase again.
6. **Integrate:** join it to neighboring material so isolated success becomes a continuous reading.
7. **Reduce assistance:** attempt with less prompting, without treating use of help as failure.
8. **Return and transfer:** revisit later and use the process on another suitable passage.

The loop is a teaching model, not eight required screens. Repetition without checking can preserve an error; checking without explanation can preserve uncertainty. Every attempt needs a plausible route to improvement.

## Progression of authentic tasks

| Task level | Typical activity | Targeted support | Useful observation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First phrase | Listen, follow words, echo | Clear model, slow replay, readable text, a simple cue | The learner knows what to try and has a way to try again |
| Short verse | Resolve the words; learn/echo phrases; join them | Known sounds, phrase controls, stress/read-form help | The learner can make an attempt containing words and melody together |
| Small passage | Prepare several related verses and their transitions | Targeted family drills, transition repetition, clear place markers | The person can turn isolated practice into a connected reading |
| Prepared passage without a leader | Attempt, compare, repair and repeat | Reference after an attempt; optional competent listener | The learner is not simply following the currently sounding model |
| Intended reading conditions | Pointed → side-by-side → unpointed as appropriate; continuous run-through and recovery | Existing tikkun controls, familiar text, prompts when needed | The learner can keep place and recover under the intended support conditions |
| Next passage | Choose a strategy and use normal resources to prepare fresh material | Optional just-in-time help, broader examples | The person can reuse a preparation process rather than only repeat one learned item |
| Later return | Revisit earlier material after a meaningful delay | Focused review of uncertain parts | Some retention and successful repair beyond immediate performance |

Do not assign a fixed completion time to these levels. Prior skills, text difficulty and support needs vary. Early authentic success can be small and meaningful without implying readiness for an entire public reading.

## Supporting strand A: usable Hebrew

The objective is to use existing decoding ability in the passage being prepared and resolve specific word/stress/read-form uncertainties. Initial alphabet/vowel instruction is not part of the core chanting course. Tool availability must become understandable action:

- Hear the intended word; use syllable/stress information while practicing it.
- Resolve the spoken form when the displayed written form differs.
- Practice the word in its phrase after working on it alone.
- Recognize when decoding help is needed and how to get it.

The optional Hebrew-fluency strand is supplementary rather than a required foundation gate for this audience. Six short units alone should not be advertised as evidence of broad Hebrew-reading fluency. A person who can decode Hebrew may still prefer ear-first preparation; the text needs to remain correctly anchored. Preserve current language-display and licensing decisions.

## Supporting strand B: sounds and signs

Keep family-recognition practice, with stronger teaching around it:

- Clear, curated anchor sound before the first classification task.
- Listen-and-echo opportunities with comfortable repetition.
- A manageable connection between sound, its written cue and actual words.
- Easy recognition among taught options, with models available.
- Deliberate later variation in words, voice, register, lead-in and context.
- Rare signs and special cases as meaningful extensions, not universal gates before a first verse.

Names and contours can be useful aids. The ability to name a sign is not a prerequisite for every valid way of learning to chant. Conversely, an ear-first learner should have a route to reusable patterns when they want it. Do not replace the current early quiz with another compulsory naming exam.

## Supporting strand C: joining and phrasing

Teach joins explicitly. A person who can produce phrase A and phrase B separately may still struggle at A→B. The same applies to verse transitions.

Use the existing playback and passage tools to teach a small sequence: establish each part, practice the transition, include the surrounding words and return to the complete unit. Repeat at the level where the difficulty actually occurs, rather than always replaying the entire passage or requiring another family-name drill.

Teach useful structural meaning with the actual text and a model. Avoid assuming a grammatical boundary is always heard as a literal silence or that a display split explains itself. Daniel should approve the musical and language cues.

## Supporting strand D: learning how to practice

Provide a repeatable preparation method rather than just controls:

1. Look over the chosen reading and hear an appropriate model.
2. Identify a manageable unit and the first unresolved difficulty.
3. Work on the words or sound that needs attention.
4. Return that work to its phrase and connect it to the next part.
5. Try without being led; compare and choose the next useful practice.
6. Make a clear stopping point and return to it later.

This is an instructional scaffold, not a new scheduling or accounting system. More practice is useful when it is targeted; a larger mandatory item count is not an adequate substitute. It should work with both public defaults and custom/shared dated readings.

## Supporting strand E: self-monitoring and feedback

Start with a small focus the learner can understand, such as a particular word, stress or phrase ending. Do not ask novices to judge every dimension simultaneously. Show how to use the reference to decide what to try next.

The system can guide attention and comparison without claiming to hear the learner. Existing tutor/rabbi feedback can supply correction when available. The program must be explicit about what an unchecked self-report means. Program evaluation can include live competent observation while public access remains tutor-optional.

No new voice recording, automatic accuracy score, generative audio or paid coaching service follows from this proposal.

## Supporting strand F: independence, retention and recovery

Fading assistance is a taught progression, not merely a toggle:

- Use colors/marks while a task is new, and try less help when useful.
- Compare a pointed text with its unpointed version for scroll preparation.
- If removing help exposes a difficulty, restore the specific aid and practice the relevant part.
- Learn a simple, teacher-approved recovery procedure for a lost place or stumble.
- Revisit complete verses and transitions after a delay, not just family labels.

Recovery and use of resources are part of competence. Do not turn independence into a demand for an unprompted perfect performance on every first attempt.

## Role of adjacent service learning

Service/prayer study supplies meaning and context and can support a person's actual service part. Torah chanting and prayer melody are not interchangeable curricula. Connect them where useful; preserve the approved service-course sequence and its audio schedule. D'var Torah and family logistics remain outside the app.

## What this implies for the present course map

The family catalogue remains a useful library and practice strand. The learner's main progression becomes increasingly independent preparation and chanting. A person may return to a family lesson from the current phrase, practice the needed sound and return to the reading. The existing home/tab/navigation work can express that structure without this review choosing new UI or changing the visual direction.

Before implementation, Claude Code should reconcile this architecture with P4/N17/N18/N19, existing reading/player tools and the Hebrew/service work. Do not implement a second set of player, progress or reader infrastructure because this document describes an instructional connection.
