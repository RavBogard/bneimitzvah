# Readiness and evidence of learning

24 October 2026. Proposed evaluation framework, not a test implemented in the app, an authorized pilot or a new ritual standard. Daniel's existing human-testing gate remains. No learner recruitment, recording, invitations, telemetry or assessment data collection is initiated.

## The question to answer

Does the program help a person who can decode pointed Hebrew acquire a usable preparation process and the ability to chant under the intended conditions? Basic Hebrew phonetic ability is the clarified entry assumption. Completing the course, recognizing examples and feeling confident are relevant observations but do not alone answer that question.

Evaluate usefulness for different starting abilities as specified in [ENTRY-POINTS-AND-SUPPORT.md](ENTRY-POINTS-AND-SUPPORT.md). New chanters, ear-experienced learners, partial/rusty trope users and experienced readers need useful entry and appropriately focused help. These profiles overlap; evaluate actual skill dimensions rather than assign a single ability rank.

Evaluate three outcomes separately:

1. **Prepared performance:** the person chants material they prepared, without a model currently leading their voice.
2. **Preparation transfer:** the person can approach another suitable passage using ordinary resources and choose useful practice without continuous step-by-step direction.
3. **Retention and intended conditions:** they can return after a delay, restore uncertain parts, and use the text/support conditions their intended reading requires, including unpointed text where applicable.

This does not require surprising novices with an unfamiliar passage and forbidding preparation. It does not assume immediate sight-reading or prohibit recordings and a tikkun as preparation resources.

## Proposed capability observations

These are dimensions for a teacher/program reviewer to consider. They are not public scores, grade labels or new fields to store. Daniel determines the relevant musical variants, acceptable correction and congregational expectations.

| Dimension | Evidence to look for | Inadequate substitute |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Text correctness | Intended words and spoken forms remain accurate in the prepared passage | A family name selected correctly |
| Pronunciation and stress | The person applies the relevant syllables/stress and resolves uncertainties appropriately | A word card was opened |
| Musical realization | Appropriate family patterns/phrasing in the chosen tradition, with a comfortable individual voice | Copying the recording's vocal timbre or selecting a contour picture |
| Integration | Words and phrases join into a coherent reading; transitions are prepared | Each isolated clip was played once |
| Independence | The person can attempt without being led by current playback, using the intended text | Chanting only in unison with the model |
| Monitoring and repair | The learner notices uncertainty, uses an appropriate reference/help and improves the next attempt | Clicking Got it or repeating indefinitely |
| Continuity and recovery | They retain/find place and recover sensibly from a stumble | Navigating to the next verse with a button |
| Transfer | They can explain and use a preparation strategy on a new suitable passage | Recognizing another random excerpt from the practice pool |
| Retention | Some capability remains after a delay and the learner can repair what faded | A strong immediate end-of-lesson performance |

Do not set a universal percentage, note-for-note threshold, fixed number of repetitions or completion time from this review. Generated-audio G1/G2/G3 criteria are not learner assessment criteria. Do not copy those gates into a readiness test.

## A progression check can also teach

For an unfamiliar but suitable preparation task, observe the help needed and how the person uses it. A useful prompt or model can reveal the next teaching need. Offer another attempt after instruction. That is more informative than treating a single difficult answer as a diagnosis of ability.

Record observations in ordinary review notes only when an authorized evaluation occurs; this document proposes no new analytics system. Looking at what support was needed is more useful than interpreting course completion as a hidden musical score.

## The self-guided feedback problem

There are three distinct questions:

- Can the learner practice without an assigned tutor? The public experience should support this.
- Can the app accurately judge the learner's voice? Under current scope, it does not.
- Can a self-guided course help people learn even without automated judgment? Plausibly, if it teaches comparison, correction and appropriate help-seeking; its outcomes still need validation.

A human can observe a live attempt without an app recording feature. Expert observation used to evaluate a program does not imply a mandatory tutor or sign-in gate for every person. Conversely, an optional tutor should not be silently doing all the teaching that a course claims to provide.

The app should be truthful: “practiced,” “reviewed” or an explicit self-assessment describes participation or the learner's judgment. A claim of demonstrated independent readiness needs corresponding evidence. No certificate, badge or public comparison is proposed.

## Proposed later evaluation sequence

### A. Program and content review with Daniel

Available now as consultation, without a new pilot:

- Check that the outcome is the right one for CRC and distinguish ordinary readings from special cases.
- Review an entire miniature preparation task, not just its separate screens.
- Verify the actual starter recordings, text, cues, joins and correction examples.
- Check that every claimed capability has teaching, practice, feedback and an appropriate later application.
- Confirm that a basic Hebrew decoder has a usable chanting route, including an ear-first route, without requiring initial-literacy lessons. Check that more experienced readers can bypass familiar work and address real gaps.

This is expert design/content acceptance. It does not prove novice learning or establish a universal completion time.

### B. Formative learner walkthroughs after the existing gate allows them

When authorized, include relevant differences in chanting experience and knowledge within the basic-Hebrew-decoding population. Observe people using the proposed route and receiving ordinary help. Include mismatched strengths, such as strong imitation with little sign knowledge or good sign knowledge with weak phrase production. Look for misunderstandings, redundant mandatory work, unproductive repetition, unclear cues and places where a supposed skill is actually being supplied by a hidden tutor prompt.

Keep actual task conditions in view: the device, sound output, chosen passage and preparation resources. The same interface can serve everyone while teaching support varies with the immediate task. Do not infer ability from age or impose a learning-style classification.

### C. Outcome and transfer checks

When authorized, compare the person's starting capability with what they can do after learning. Include a prepared passage, another suitable preparation task and a delayed return. Examine benefits and failures by starting profile as well as overall results: efficient targeted preparation may matter most for experienced readers, while first independent production may matter most for new chanters. Competent musical/textual review should be distinct from the app's own completion claims. If feasible, avoid the reviewer relying on those claims while listening.

No particular participant count or experimental design is mandated here. Small formative evaluations find problems; they do not prove population-wide effectiveness. Broader product claims need evidence appropriate to their breadth. A formal comparison study is an option for stronger claims, not a prerequisite this consultation invents for ordinary development.

## Program-level failure signals

- Learners can name families but cannot produce them from usable text.
- They can copy one recording but have no method for preparing another passage.
- Pronunciation/text errors persist because the course never helps detect them.
- Removing continuous playback collapses performance despite lesson completion.
- They can perform isolated phrases but fail at joins or lose place in a passage.
- The foundation route is insufficient or unavailable for a group the product promises to serve.
- An expert must repeatedly supply essential instruction absent from the supposed self-guided pathway.
- Immediate success is mistaken for durable learning.

These indicate a teaching/program problem to investigate, not a reason to blame the learner or simply increase mandatory retries.

## Current conclusion

The evidence examined establishes technical assets and teaching intentions, plus concrete usability problems. It does not establish that the product has achieved the full novice-to-capable outcome. A redesigned learning architecture can make that outcome more plausible; appropriate later observation is needed to establish how well it works and for whom.
