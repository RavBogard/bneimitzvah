# Proposed teaching sequence: preparing and chanting Torah

24 October 2026. First curriculum design draft under Daniel's authorization to work alongside 1.9/2.0. Basic pointed-Hebrew decoding is assumed. This translates the structural review into teachable units and acceptance criteria; it does not implement or supersede Claude's release plan.

## The instructional contract

Help a person prepare appropriate Torah with ordinary resources, chant the prepared passage without a sounding model leading them, repair common problems and reuse the preparation process. Scroll preparation adds unpointed reading where relevant. No promise of instant sight-reading, comprehensive special-melody expertise or a fixed completion time.

Every core unit connects a real text to an action, a model, an independent attempt and a useful way to check or repair. Family recognition and terminology support that work. They do not define the learner's entire progress.

## Entry and pacing

Use the flexible [entry profiles](ENTRY-POINTS-AND-SUPPORT.md): a new chanter starts with a phrase; an ear-experienced learner may start with a verse; a rusty reader can start with a passage and targeted refresher; an experienced reader can go straight to their reading or a focused topic. These are suggestions, not hard unlocks or permanent levels.

Apply support by dimension. A fluent imitator may need sign explanations, and a person who knows every name may need echoing and joins. Familiar work is bypassable. Optional self-report or a short supported attempt informs recommendations without claiming the app heard or graded a voice.

## The main sequence

| Unit | What the person learns to do | Teaching and practice | Check/repair and next use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Make a first phrase | Follow the words and make a first sung attempt | Hear a short, comfortable authentic phrase; follow active word outline; read uncertain words; echo at a useful pace | Attend to the words and the ending separately; replay just the difficult part; end with an attempt without a leading voice |
| 2. Learn a useful ending | Hear, imitate and reuse the phrase ending in that reading | Model the ending; practice its actual words; optionally name/show its sign and family | Return the ending to the whole phrase. Easy recognition is offered only after its alternatives are taught |
| 3. Prepare a first verse | Learn manageable parts and join them | Hear the whole verse for orientation; practice parts; bridge their boundary; return to the complete verse | Attempt without playback; compare a specific uncertainty; repair and try in context. Introduce a second anchor sound when the chosen text needs it |
| 4. Prepare a small passage | Extend the same process across verses | Preview a manageable reading; identify uncertain words/patterns; learn units; practice verse transitions | Distinguish local difficulty from a joining problem. Rehearse the complete passage after targeted work |
| 5. Manage preparation | Decide what practice is useful and when to return | Plan a small goal; make an attempt; identify a need; choose an aid; stop at a clear return point | Teach the preparation method explicitly rather than count clicks. Offer relevant family/word/transition practice and return to the passage |
| 6. Reduce assistance and recover | Chant a prepared passage without a leading voice under intended conditions | Attempt with less prompting; use available pointed/marks/tikkun states as relevant; model a teacher-approved recovery procedure | Restore the specific aid when needed; repair locally and retry. Removing color or changing view is not itself proof of readiness |
| 7. Return and prepare another reading | Retain work and transfer the preparation method | Revisit earlier phrases/joins after a delay; prepare fresh suitable material with normal resources | Assess what was retained, what repair helped and how the next preparation was managed; do not substitute another label quiz |

Units are capabilities, not seven fixed-length sessions or a demand to finish one before seeing the next. Units 4–7 recur as readings become more demanding. The smallest complete example is specified in [WORKED-FIRST-READING.md](WORKED-FIRST-READING.md).

## The family library inside this sequence

Keep the existing Torah family lessons, exemplars and chart as a supporting library. Sof pasuk and etnachta are useful early anchors when the example supports them. A beginner's first practical verse should not wait until all ten listed Torah family lessons are finished. If the chosen first task needs another pattern, teach that pattern in context rather than present an unexplained exception.

For each family, use the same authoring pattern:

1. A clear accurate anchor in the chosen tradition, initially with a stable model voice.
2. A short attention cue describing what to listen for, musically checked by Daniel.
3. Echoing with the actual words, at a comfortable pace.
4. The relevant sign/mark connection with optional terminology depth.
5. Easy recognition using already-taught options and replayable models.
6. Production from text, then application in a phrase and a join.
7. Later examples that deliberately vary words, position or voice, with the reason explained.

Not every family needs every activity on every visit. Distinguish a family pattern from copying someone's vocal timbre. Do not reduce structural phrasing to a universal instruction to insert a silence.

Rare forms, look-alikes, complex sign sequences and special endings belong in contextual help or optional depth. A learner should encounter them when relevant without having to master them before a first ordinary passage. Retain legitimate musical variation and route to the correct tradition; Daniel reviews actual musical cues.

## What happens to current exercise types

| Current type | Proposed role |
| --- | --- |
| Hear it / In verses | Keep as model and contextual explanation; add an explicit attempt and useful attention cue |
| Same/different | Remove from the opening; optional later listening/generalization after the relevant sounds are taught, with curated examples. No universal gate |
| Hear/name | Keep easy supported recognition after teaching; names should not block an otherwise valid ear-first path |
| Endpoint | Use after learners know the sound and the question's purpose; distinguish hearing from applying a known pattern without audio |
| Tap phrase / which half | Teach phrase structure in actual text first; use as relevant practice rather than an opening abstraction |
| Build sign sequence / look-alikes | Keep as optional sign/structure depth, useful for learners who need it; never equate tile accuracy with vocal competence |
| Chant-along | Keep as supported production, followed by an attempt without a sounding leader |
| Your turn | Make the attempt useful through a specific comparison focus and repair route; record only what the user actually reports under existing contracts |
| Quick ten / review cards | Keep focused recognition practice; do not let it stand in for delayed phrase/verse production and passage review |
| Run-through | Use to find local practice needs, continuity and recovery; maintain current privacy and no-tally decisions |

## Observable acceptance criteria

These are design-review questions and future authorized learning observations, not automatic pass/fail tests in the app:

- A basic Hebrew decoder can reach meaningful singing without a vocabulary exam or full family catalogue.
- Each introduced sound is modeled and practiced before a question requires discriminating it.
- A learner has a way to work on a specific word, sound or transition and return to the whole reading.
- An independent attempt is available and is distinct from singing along; the app does not claim to assess its accuracy.
- Learners at different starting levels can reach useful work, bypass redundancy and return to explanations.
- A prepared passage leads to a repeatable preparation method and another passage, not only a completion screen.
- Scroll practice and recovery are taught where relevant rather than inferred from a toggle.
- Retention and fresh preparation are examined separately from immediate success and lesson completion.

## Scope and integration

The same structure works with the public default, an owner's dated reading or an open shared reading. Private progress/profile information does not travel with a public content link. Do not require a ceremony record, account or tutor before public learning.

Haftarah should have direct entry and its own reviewed melodic models; no unnecessary Torah prerequisite is proposed. This draft works through Torah first because it is the requested outcome. It does not claim every Torah cue transfers musically to haftarah.

The 2.0 service course retains its approved teachings, prayer sequence, optional Hebrew strand and audio schedule. No new prayer melody, generated chant, learner recording, automatic vocal score, schema, persistence engine or shared player is authorized by this design.
