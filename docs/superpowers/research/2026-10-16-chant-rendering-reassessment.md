# Chant voice rendering reassessment — 2026-10-16

Research for Astra's integration decision. No model downloaded, inference/training run, service provisioned, money spent, voice uploaded, or learner release performed. The one hardware check was read-only `nvidia-smi`: **RTX 4070 Laptop GPU, 8,188 MiB VRAM** on this host. Prior measurements below are inherited evidence, not rerun results.

## Recommendation

**Do not designate a winning renderer from the literature.** No reviewed primary source establishes accurate Biblical Hebrew cantillation on arbitrary assigned verses. Keep the deterministic Hebrew/score layer, make the renderer replaceable, and select by a small, identical teaching-quality benchmark. The present SoulX implementation is a useful control, not a settled optimum.

With Daniel now willing to record or recruit a willing cantor/tutor, a **Hebrew-trained, explicitly controlled singing model deserves a real feasibility pilot**. DiffSinger is the strongest architectural fit reviewed for that experiment: actual Hebrew phones, supplied durations, and supplied F0 rather than hoping another language's tokens realize Hebrew. That is a fit judgment, **not evidence that a small custom bank will sound better**. Neither the sufficient recording duration nor the training cost is established.

Use three candidate lanes: **A, current SoulX with corrected phrase timing; B, native-Hebrew audio into score-controlled neural resynthesis; C, a consented Hebrew DiffSinger acoustic-model pilot.** A/B can be compared before training C. A direct human chant is the reference and practical fallback. A brief Synthesizer V trial is a useful optional challenger if A/B fail and C's data preparation looks disproportionate; it should not silently become the default or an ML training-data source.

This is a research recommendation, not an amendment to the mission. Daniel's G1/G2/G3 approval remains required before any student hears generated chant. User-facing transliteration is unrelated to internal phoneme control and is not required for these routes.

## What exists, and what the previous reports overstate

Targeted inspection: `trope/render-cli.mjs`, `trope/modal_render.py`, `trope/soulx_runner.py`, `trope/soulx.mjs`, `scripts/trope-render.mjs`; also `trope/speech2sing.mjs`, `trope/speech2sing.py`, `trope/soulx_svc_runner.py`, `trope/s2s_pitch.py` and their driver. Existing evidence includes `2026-10-15-trope-engine/voice-feasibility.md`, `voice-quality.md`, and `speech2sing-experiment.md`.

- **Implemented main render CLI:** SoulX SVS with two execution locations, `local` and `modal`. Modal reuses the same Python load/render functions, pinned source commit, and resident model. Changing GPU location does not introduce a new voice model or solve diction. This runner calls `cli.inference`.
- **Implemented separate S2S experiment:** `scripts/trope-speech2sing.mjs` drives Phonikud-TTS, duration-controlled speech, WORLD comparisons and `soulx_svc_runner.py` (which does call `cli.inference_svc`). `speech2sing.py` derives ONNX graphs to expose/replace predicted duration frames, generates longer vowels inside the decoder, preserves consonant spans with a 60%-span cap, and writes alignment plus score F0 with voiceless regions masked. This is more than a proposed adapter. The experiment note records raw/timed TTS on **two verses**, noisy ASR measurements, and a tiny two-step/four-second CPU SVC smoke test. The full SVC chant/listening/pitch comparison was **not completed**. DiffSinger/training remain proposals.
- **Implemented controls:** syllable or whole-word tokens, stress digits, word/phrase segmentation, alternate timing, score or synthetic-F0 conditioning, steps/guidance and optional loudness processing. Default CLI style remains **v1** (word gaps, grid rhythm, lexical stress, syllable tokens, score control); v2 changes phrasing only. Consequently, "we have already optimized legato" is not equivalent to "all normal renders use the legato settings."
- **Diction limitation:** the adapter maps Hebrew phones to English ARPAbet and some whole Mandarin syllables. Changing the written IPA upstream cannot create a phone the trained acoustic model does not know. Splitting words into syllable tokens may also affect transitions; grouping words changes that tradeoff and needs testing.
- **Evidence:** the old feasibility report measured a short verse on a GTX 1660 Ti, including 33/33 measured notes within ±0.5 semitone and about 86 seconds for 14.25 seconds of audio. This supports pitch/short-inference feasibility on that setup. It does not prove Hebrew consonants, stress, arbitrary-verse coverage, longer passages, or performance on the present laptop. The instrumental analysis itself explicitly deferred those judgments to listening.
- **Reproducibility:** the reviewed wrapper exposes no seed setting or seed manifest field. A fixed score is deterministic as an instruction, but repeat neural renders and different GPU numerics need not be identical waveforms. Pin source/model/config/seed where supported, and retain the selected approved artifact.

Corrections to `voice-quality.md`: (1) the proposed full TTS→SVC chain has not earned first-place quality status; (2) supplying target F0 is conditioning, not proof of pitch or word correctness; (3) SVC is designed to retain source content, but "whatever consonant went in must come out unchanged" is too strong—conversion may degrade it; (4) the actual duration-controlled speech experiment does not establish natural sung vowels—the note reports slightly worse pooled ASR CER for timed versus raw speech, with large take-to-take variation and no human listening yet; (5) a 15–30-minute Hebrew DiffSinger dataset and a two-weekend delivery are not established requirements or estimates; (6) excluding a current TTS release on a secondary 2025 snapshot is insufficient. The same experiment note says Daniel accepted Phonikud's NC use as an intermediate; preserve that authorization, but conversion replacing timbre is not evidence that restrictive source-license obligations disappear. Review exact intended use against the full wording before release.

## The benchmark that can decide

Prepare **12 short, private, rights-cleared test passages**, with Daniel confirming the intended pronunciation and melody first. This is a proposed pilot size, not a statistical release qualification. Cover ordinary syllabic chanting; weak/voiced sheva and hataf; final consonants; ח/כ, ה, צ, ר; maqaf/stress; qamatz qatan; vowel-initial joins; a long held vowel; multiple-note melisma; rapid repeated notes; a major disjunctive breath; and a long verse requiring a safe phrase split. Include new word/phone contexts absent from any pilot training set. No licensed passage text or audio enters the public repo.

1. **A — SoulX control:** phrase-timed settings, explicit reviewed phones, clean consented sung prompt. Start with score mode and compare supplied-F0 mode on only the cases where transition control matters. One fixed documented configuration per scored condition, not unlimited cherry-picking.
2. **B — native Hebrew source + SVC:** first use a short consenting spoken Hebrew recording for diagnostic isolation, then a rights-cleared Hebrew TTS for the arbitrary-verse test. Align and preserve consonant spans; extend vowel nuclei only; supply the same intended score F0 to SoulX-SVC. Keep unconverted and DSP-only audio as controls. If TTS already changes a word/stress, reject before SVC; if SVC drops a consonant, that is a failure. This is an experiment, not a claimed fix.
3. **C — Hebrew DiffSinger pilot:** first audit a small clean recording sample for all target phones, usable pitch range and labeling effort. A further controlled recording set and training run follow only if that audit supports the path. Train acoustic rendering with explicit phoneme durations/F0 first; do not require a learned pitch predictor to test the renderer. Add learned durations/expressive curves only after held-out phone/melisma correctness. No assertion that 20 minutes, a particular epoch count, or 8 GB VRAM guarantees training success.

Match key, target note sequence, phrase length, loudness and listening order. Include blind human reference files. Judge normal speed and the intended slow practice speed. Document any manual repairs and human minutes per minute of accepted chant; a beautiful one-off edited verse may be a poor arbitrary-assignment pipeline.

**Falsifiable selection rule:** reject a lane on any word/syllable/stress or "would teach wrong" failure, even if its timbre is preferred. Compare accepted lanes by Daniel's blind diction/connectedness ratings, then repair burden and measured cost. If spoken-human B succeeds but automatic-TTS B fails, the conversion route is promising but arbitrary-verse automation is not solved. If neither A nor B passes, proceed with C's data audit or the bounded commercial trial rather than indefinitely tuning failed outputs. If C also fails held-out passages, retain human per-verse recording; do not relabel the least-bad model as teaching-ready. Training C is not a prerequisite for shipping the existing authorized human-recording route.

| Check | Proposed pilot rejection / acceptance evidence |
|---|---|
| Words and phonemes | **Zero** inserted, omitted or substituted words/syllables; no misheard word caused by a consonant/vowel error; zero incorrect teaching pronunciation. Log exact time and phone. Daniel's transcript/listening judgment is authoritative; ASR is only a diagnostic. |
| Stress and text-to-note mapping | Every intended stress audible in the correct syllable; every note group attached to the correct vowel nucleus. No vowel reattack or extra syllable on slurs unless intended. Record a count and reason for every failure. |
| Melody / G1 | Apply existing ±50-cent G1 to stable voiced note interiors with octave-error checks and pitch-tracker confidence. Compare note identity/order/count and final cadence separately. Do not pass a missing brief note merely because median F0 is near the score. |
| Transitions and melisma | Plot target and extracted F0 at joins; inspect 20–100 ms around note changes as a diagnostic window, not an automatic universal tolerance. Daniel confirms intended direction, audible destination, no wrong intervening note and preserved consonants. Annotate planned glides separately from note interiors. |
| Human line | Daniel rates connectedness, clear diction and suitability for a beginner separately; require no "would teach wrong" before preference ranking. Report scores rather than claiming naturalness from model size or a vendor demo. |
| Technical / repeatability | G2 plus full-verse completion, no clipping/dropouts, reproducible provenance, peak VRAM, render wall time, failures and three repeat runs on representative difficult cases. Compare retry variance; cache approved output. |
| Release | Existing G3 remains **≥95% melody passes and zero "would teach wrong"**, after G1/G2, on the agreed release set. A 12-passage pilot winner is not a learner release. Generated-chant release still needs independent adversarial gate review. |

## Recording and compute commitments

Before recording, settle only consequential inputs: target pronunciation/nusach and acceptable realization of ח/ר/ע; comfortable key/range and tempo; Daniel versus willing cantor/tutor; consent scope (playback/prompt versus training and model use); and whether clean pre-existing recordings can be used. No student voice is needed. Begin with a short dry mono sample and the human reference passages; do not ask for hours until coverage/alignment has been inspected. Avoid promising that a reverberant existing recording can be cleaned into training-quality data.

**Inference differs from training.** An existing 8 GB-compatible checkpoint can plausibly render short segments sequentially on this laptop, but the old GTX measurement is not a present 4070 benchmark and does not establish SVC/Chatterbox/combined residency. Load stages separately if needed. DiffSinger OpenUtau can infer on CPU or Windows DirectML; training uses a separate PyTorch workflow. Batch size, segment duration, model/config and optimizer memory determine training fit; profile a bounded pilot before choosing cloud hardware. [OpenUtau runtime documentation](https://openutau.com/DiffSinger-support/).

Cloud is useful for memory/capacity after the pipeline works. It cannot correct an inappropriate phoneme inventory. ## Architecture decision to carry forward

Keep **reviewed Hebrew → pronunciation/stress → trope score** as the source of truth. Introduce a model-neutral performance object carrying stable word/syllable IDs, intended phones, note groups, durations, rests/breaths, and optional F0/energy curves. Renderer adapters may consume it as score metadata, DiffSinger DS, a commercial project, or aligned source audio. Verify adapter output against those IDs before judging waveform quality. Human recordings should remain first-class outputs with their own provenance.

Do not let a generative model infer trope from unpointed text, replace the score with a free-form musical prompt, or silently correct text through speech normalization. Conversely, do not force every model to accept SoulX's token schema: ARPAbet and Mandarin substitutes belong in the SoulX adapter. Learn phrasing from human chant where useful; keep target note identities reviewable. This preserves arbitrary-verse generation and permits changing the acoustic renderer now without changing the teaching contract.

Open evidence needed before selection: direct Hebrew listening comparisons; exact selected weight/vocoder rights; prompt/corpus consent; pilot phoneme coverage and annotation burden; measured inference/training memory and time; and held-out quality. Literature and product demos cannot supply those local results.
