# Trope engine: prior art in other traditions, labeling and alignment, evaluation, consent update

Researched 2026-10-15. Read-only; nothing else in the repo was changed. Companion to `../2026-10-15-web-2-0/chant-audio.md` (sections 1, 2(d), 4, 5 of that report are assumed and not repeated). Other agents cover the trope grammar, the Hebrew text pipeline and the voice-model survey.

Marking convention: **[V]** = verified against the linked source during this research; **[I]** = inferred by me from verified facts, or from general knowledge not re-checked today. Where a source could not be fetched (403/401/DNS) I say so.

Bottom line, in four lines:
1. No tradition has a working "notation in, reciter's voice out" system. The two closest things are a 2010 Sanskrit concatenative chanter (per-metre pitch tables plus ~2,000 recorded syllable units, i.e. our "path 3") and a 2026 Sanskrit clone built on a modern TTS backbone from ~3–5 h of one speaker, which reached expert MOS ~4.6 but **cannot be steered by notation** because its melody is implicit. The lesson: pick an architecture where pitch is an explicit input (concatenation/PSOLA or a DiffSinger-style SVS), not a TTS clone.
2. The best labeling precedent is Jewish: Ben-Shalom/Keshet (2014) ran a rule-based nikud-to-phoneme converter and a chant-adapted forced aligner over 1950s Yemenite Torah readings, trained on **one hand-labeled chapter**, and got 83% of phone boundaries within 50 ms. Modern singing aligners (MMS blend, SOFA/HubertFA) land in the same range (~75% within 50 ms) and systematically **shorten long vowels**, which is exactly where trope melismas live. Plan on a hand-labeled seed set and a correction pass.
3. Evaluation should be three independent gates: (a) melody, from pitch extraction (RMVPE/SwiftF0) scaled to the cantor's two structural pitches and aligned to the expected motif with the ISMIR-2011 alignment, calibrated against his own take-to-take variance; (b) identity, speaker-embedding cosine with controls and EQ/duration matching; (c) the cantor's blind ABX plus pass/fail with reason codes. A fixed 30-verse regression suite re-rendered per engine change.
4. Consent/law: NO FAKES stalled on the Senate floor 2026-09-30, still not law; Missouri still has only common-law publicity rights and its 2026 deepfake bill did not become law; YouTube requires the synthetic label if clips go there. The prior report's three-grant license remains the right template.

## 1. Prior art: text-to-chant in other liturgical traditions

### 1.1 Quranic recitation (tajweed-governed, melodically improvised within maqam)

**What exists**
- **Unit-selection TTS with a tajweed expert system (Bettayeb & Guerti, ~2019–2020).** Pre-selects recorded units with rules from Arabic/Quranic phonetics, then standard unit selection. Reported ~97% intelligibility and ~72% naturalness in listening tests. No reciter identity control. **[V]** https://www.researchgate.net/publication/348064369_Speech_Synthesis_System_for_the_Holy_Quran_Recitation
- **Tajweed-informed Arabic TTS front-end** (G2P and prosody rules from Quran phonetics; goal was better general Arabic TTS, not a recitation synthesizer). **[V]** https://www.researchgate.net/publication/299337545_Arabic_text_to_speech_synthesis_using_Quran-based_natural_language_processing_module
- **A minimal phonetic set for Quran recitation** (Hafs conventions, exception dictionary; built for compression, reusable as a phoneme inventory). **[V]** https://www.academia.edu/64865299/Towards_A_Minimal_Phonetic_Set_for_Quran_Recitation
- **2022–2026 research mass is recognition, not generation:** end-to-end recitation ASR (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.07034), pretrained-transformer comparison for Quranic ASR (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.19747), large-audio-model recognition (https://doi.org/10.3390/app15179521), pronunciation-error detection for learners (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.00094), reciter-style classification by multimodal fusion (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1110866525000337). **[V]** I found **no** paper or product (2022–2026) that generates an arbitrary ayah in a named reciter's voice from small data. Two targeted searches came back empty; treat the gap as real but not proven. **[I]**
- **Datasets that would make it possible:** verse-plus-reciter corpora on Hugging Face (MohamedRashad/Quran-Recitations; Muno459/QuranLab-Recitation-TTS, word-range labeled) and one corpus that is itself synthetic (rabah2026/Quran_TTS, generated with Google TTS, 19 voices). A 2024 crowdsourcing paper notes "each research project independently developing its own database." **[V]** https://huggingface.co/datasets/MohamedRashad/Quran-Recitations , https://huggingface.co/datasets/Muno459/QuranLab-Recitation-TTS , https://huggingface.co/datasets/rabah2026/Quran_TTS , https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.02675
- **Religious-authority posture (the most transferable part):** Egypt's Dar al-Ifta fatwa of **18 January 2026** permits AI-generated Quran recitations *if* they follow tajweed and are "reviewed and approved by the official bodies that verify Mushafs and Quranic recitations," do not infringe IP, and do not undermine the text's sanctity; "means take the ruling of their ends." **[V]** https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/24202/the-use-of-artificial-intelligence-in-the-creation-of-electronic-mushafs-and-ai-g . Al-Azhar separately condemned AI "Quranic songs" that set verses to music. **[V]** https://www.egyptindependent.com/al-azhar-issues-statement-forbidding-ai-generated-quranic-songs/

**Lessons for us:** the acceptability test in the sister tradition is *rule-compliance plus authority sign-off*, which maps directly onto "melodically correct per the trope grammar plus the cantor's sign-off." Nobody has solved reciter-identity synthesis there either, despite far more data; data exists but is fragmented and un-aligned. Don't expect a Quran-domain model to borrow.

### 1.2 Sanskrit and Vedic chant

**(a) Rama & Lakshmanan, "Text-to-Tuneful-Speech Synthesis of Sanskrit Verse," IJCSNS 10(1), Jan 2010 (arXiv 1409.4169).** This is our "path 3" (concatenation), done 16 years ago. **[V]** (full text read via pdftotext) https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.4169
- Melody: **per-metre pitch arrays** `p[]` stored for every metre class (1–26 syllables per quarter), relative to the note *pa* = 0. Example (Anuṣṭup, 8 syllables/quarter): quarters 1,3 = `0 1 1 2 2 0 1 1`; quarters 2,4 = `0 1 -1 0 0 1 1 1`. Indravajrā family (11/quarter): `0 0 1 2 2 0 0 1 -1 0 -1` / `0 1 0 0 0 0 -1 0 1 1 1`.
- Rhythm: syllables classed laghu/guru (1 vs 2 time units) by metre rules; a unit that is short where the metre wants long is **stretched** (or silence inserted at a word end); one time unit of silence at the caesura.
- Voice: a pre-recorded database of **~2,000 syllabic unit clips** (pronounceable units parsed by rule from a dictionary sweep; all laghu units recorded in one time unit); each unit is pitch-shifted by `f(u_i, p_i)` using Audacity/DirectX APIs, then streamed into one WAV.
- Evaluation: none reported beyond "reasonably well"; the authors note accuracy "is dependent on the tone and time duration of the recorded audio units" and that they had to re-record units for pitch and duration during testing.
- Lesson: a rule-table melody plus ~2k recorded units is a working design, and the failure mode they hit (units recorded at the wrong length/pitch) argues for recording each unit **at the pitch and length the table will ask for**, i.e. record motifs in context, not isolated syllables.

**(b) Vāgdhenu, "A Vrutta (Meter) Aware Shloka-to-Chant (TTS) System for Sanskrit," arXiv 2608.26146 (submitted 2026-06-28; experience report).** The strongest recent "small data, one voice, chant" case. **[V]** https://arxiv.org/html/2608.26146 , code https://github.com/prathoshap/vagdhenu , weights/data https://huggingface.co/prathoshap
- Data: single speaker (the author), controlled room, 24-bit/48 kHz downsampled to 24 kHz; released ~1,467 clips ≈ **5.3 h** (primary style ≈ 3.0 h, 764 verses, ~33,700 syllables); each quarter-verse one breath group.
- Backbone: IndicF5 (F5-TTS flow-matching DiT, 337M) + BigVGAN-v2; earlier eras StyleTTS2 (MOS 3.0–4.2), VITS2, Matcha-TTS (4.2–4.3). "The backbone, not the data, was the bottleneck."
- **Melody is implicit.** The model has no pitch or duration head; "the reference clip carries voice, melodic contour, and pace." A text-side prosody conditioner was "architecturally inert" (gradient norm ~0.02). "Vrutta-aware" means the detected metre picks a matched *reference recording* and a duration budget and caesura; it is not a learned melody.
- Evaluation: expert single-listener MOS ≈ 4.6 (humans 4.6–4.7); pitch correlation 0.13 for same-reference/different-verse vs 0.40–0.54 for different-reference/same-verse; no multi-listener study, no speaker-similarity score.
- Lessons listed by the authors: reference-clip choice (a clean ~7 s half-hemistich) drives prosody; vocoder choice not interchangeable (Fourier vocoder introduced a long-vowel phase artifact); **forced-alignment QC was "low-signal at scale"** (high false positives) and they switched to duration gates and source-text validation; audible word gaps in a pilot taught the model to read spaces as pauses; published video can't be edited in place, so catch errors pre-publication. Model card: "No Vedic svaras."
- Lesson for us: a 3–5 h single-speaker clone of chant can sound excellent, but a TTS clone **cannot be told which trope to sing**. If the melody is to be computed from te'amim, pitch must be an explicit model input (SVS) or explicit signal processing (concatenation/PSOLA). Vāgdhenu is still useful as a recording-protocol and QA template.

**(c) Vedic accent (udātta/anudātta/svarita):** no synthesizer with explicit svara control found. The text side exists (accent-mark placement for Rigvedic text, C-DAC, BHASHA 2025: https://aclanthology.org/2025.bhasha-1.11). **[V]** A low-resource Sanskrit Tacotron2 TTS (arXiv 2212.03558) used external chant recordings but is speech, not chant. **[V]**

### 1.3 Byzantine chant (psaltic neumes, microtonal modes)

- **No neural or research system renders audio from neumes.** What exists: **BZQ** (P. Katsoulis, free Windows, builds dated 2014–2015), which reads a BZQ-encoded score or solfège text, translates to European notes, and "pronounces the note syllables with a human voice" (voices such as "SOLFEGE"), with "quality" signs (petasti, flutter, yodel) applied at selectable intensity. Whether the voices are samples or synthesis is not stated. **[V]** https://bzquality.wordpress.com/ , https://analogion.com/site/html/Technology.html . **Melodos** (commercial, S. Papadopoulos) writes and plays back scores. **[V]** **Neanes** (open-source scorewriter) aligns neumes to lyrics and computes martyriai but produces scores, not audio. **[V]** https://neanes.github.io/neanes/
- Encoding/OCR: Dalitz et al. 2008 optical recognition (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10032-008-0074-4); Bouris et al. 2018 Python encoding project (early stage). **[V]**
- Analysis corpus: **DAMASKINOS** pairs chanter recordings with notation, *expected pitch* and *performed pitch* layers, synchronized semi-automatically (mentioned in the WoRMS 2024 proceedings, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.15741). **[V]**
- Lesson: the only working notation-to-audio tools are solfège-syllable players, i.e. TropeTrainer-class. The DAMASKINOS "expected vs performed pitch" split is precisely the evaluation framing we need (section 5).

### 1.4 Gregorian/Latin chant (square notation, gabc)

- **Score side is mature:** GregoBase holds 9,000+ gabc transcriptions; chant21/PyCantus parse them; Gregorio typesets to PDF only. A desktop GUI ("Greg") can emit MIDI from gabc but a 2025 forum thread shows it failing on some fragments. **[V]** https://gregoriochant.org/ , https://forum.musicasacra.com/forum/discussion/22154 , https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.11933
- **CantusSVS** (Liam Pond, Université de Montréal course project MUS6329X): a Hugging Face Space that synthesizes Latin chant from a custom score with **DiffSinger**, controlling melody, rhythm, phonemes and timing; pitch range D4–D5; phoneme list includes `ap` (pause) and `br` (breath). The Space's page itself returned 401 for me; details come from its app.py as indexed. **[V-partial]** https://huggingface.co/spaces/liampond/CantusSVS-hf/blob/main/app.py
- Consumer "AI Gregorian chant" (Soundverse, Tunee, "Chant GPT" on streaming) is prompt-to-music with no notation input; Catholic commentary allows it for study, not worship. **[V]** https://ewtn.co.uk/article-chant-gpt-how-catholics-are-responding-to-ai-generated-gregorian-chant/
- Lesson: a DiffSinger voicebank with a tiny custom phoneme set and a one-octave range is a feasible course-project-scale deliverable when the melody is explicit. That is the architecture for our "path 1."

### 1.5 Buddhist sutra chanting, Coptic, Syriac

- Nothing found for synthesis in any of the three. Adjacent: Metta Voice (open-source sutta reader using commercial TTS, no chant), a Taiwanese October 2025 essay framing "AI chanting" philosophically, Syriac *vocalization* models (text only), and a 2025 physiology study of shōmyō breathing. **[V]** https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/api/v1/projects/github.com%2Fbetmig%2Fmettavoice , https://human.lawtw.com/en/news/20251029222610384dc2 , https://johnlockejrr-marianmt-syr-voc-western.hf.space
- Older general work: IRCAM's CHANT singing synthesis (1984) and a real-time Greek chant synthesis paper (AOIDOS) are formant-era and not notation-driven. **[V-title only]** https://www.ee.columbia.edu/~dpwe/papers/RodetPB84-CHANT.pdf , https://www.academia.edu/1375677/Real_Time_Control_of_Greek_Chant_Synthesis

### 1.6 Cross-tradition computational analysis (the Biró / van Kranenburg line)

- ICMPC 2012, "Stability and Variation in Cadence Formulas in Oral and Semi-Oral Chant Traditions" (Biró, van Kranenburg, Ness, Tzanetakis, Volk), pp. 98–105: compares Torah trope, Quran recitation and plainchant cadences. Not retrieved; cited in the TENOR paper. **[V-citation only]**
- TENOR 2024, "Divine Rationality: Computational Transcription of Chants…" (Biró & van Kranenburg, CC BY 4.0): YIN pitch, 40 ms windows / 10 ms hop, MIDI with cent precision; recordings of **the same reciter over several years** to test memory vs melodic stability; a method that separates "skeletal" scale pitches from "ornamental" ones. **[V]** https://www.tenor-conference.org/proceedings/2024/21_TENOR2024_Biro.pdf
- Lesson: the stable, teachable part of a chant is the skeletal pitch hierarchy; ornaments vary even within one reciter. Our melodic-correctness metric should score the skeleton strictly and the ornaments loosely (section 5.2).

### 1.7 Transferable lessons (ranked)

1. **Explicit melody or nothing.** Every working notation-to-audio system (Sanskrit 2010, BZQ, CantusSVS, TropeTrainer) takes pitch as an explicit input. Every impressive small-data voice clone (Vāgdhenu) has implicit melody and can't be steered. Choose concatenation/PSOLA or DiffSinger-class SVS for the engine; use TTS-style clones only for a spoken voice. **[I]**
2. **Record the units the engine will ask for.** Rama & Lakshmanan had to re-record units whose length/pitch didn't fit; Vāgdhenu's model learned pauses from word gaps. Record motifs in context, at the tempo and register the engine will use, with deliberate silences only where the engine will cut. **[I]**
3. **A single hand-labeled chapter is enough to bootstrap an aligner** (Ben-Shalom/Keshet 2014; section 3). **[V]**
4. **Alignment QC is noisy; gate on durations and text instead** (Vāgdhenu). **[V]**
5. **Authority sign-off is the acceptability test** in the sister traditions (Dar al-Ifta 2026). Build the cantor review into the pipeline, not after it. **[V]**
6. **Skeleton vs ornament.** Score the structural pitches strictly; tolerate ornament variance (ISMIR 2011, TENOR 2024). **[V]**
7. **Catch errors before publishing** (Vāgdhenu: "published video can't be edited in place"); same applies to PDFs and cached audio on the sheet site. **[V]**

## 2. TropeTrainer's method in detail

### 2.1 Buchler / Kinnor (1999–2019)

**Engine.** DECtalk (Klatt formant synthesizer; Fonix 5.1 era), licensed by Buchler; Stacy/Stacey Schnee guided him into it around 1999. DECtalk supported English, Spanish and German, so the first Hebrew phoneme set was assembled from "some English and a couple of German sounds." Schnee and Edward Bruckert later built a dedicated Hebrew phoneme set and added pauses for natural cadence. DECtalk "could also play phonemes at specific musical pitches," and Buchler "put all the phonemes in with pitches and duration." Buchler also added Hebrew Unicode input and the letter-pronunciation rules. **[V]** https://www.inverse.com/input/features/tropetrainer-thomas-buchler-torah-software

**Who supplied the melody.** Rabbi Yaakov Zucker taught Buchler the trope; Cantor Neil Schwartz "helped notate the trope melodies, turned the notation into DECtalk code, and created a more legible Hebrew font." Buchler "created the rules for trope himself." Traditions grew from 7 to 29 (Binder, Jacobson, Moroccan, Yerushalmi, Chabad among them). Users could adjust timbre and speed "from unbearably slow to comfortably fast." **[V]** (same source)

**What the files look like (from someone who opened it).** HN user *jes5199*: the pieces are "an encoding of the Hebrew text, an XML file that describes the musical notes for different 'tropes' (in several traditions), an INI file mapping Hebrew letters to DECtalk phonemes (in several dialects)," plus "very gnarly hand-written logic for Hebrew pronunciation rules" ("lots of switch statements"); the output to DECtalk is "just strings of ascii phoneme names and pitch and duration information"; binaries are Xojo/REALbasic; the DECtalk DLLs run under Wine; later versions phone home for activation (DRM). *Animats*: "probably mostly a DECtalk player and a big collection of playable religious DECtalk files." *datajake1999*: the DECtalk source for the version used was released. **[V, but anecdotal]** https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=35401895

**DECtalk side.** The leaked Fonix DECtalk 5.1 source (RetroBunn/dt51 fork) lists Hebrew `[:lang hb]` with the note "Hebrew did not have any letter to sound (LTS) rules," phonemes only, and "Hebrew was made for TropeTrainer." The repository warns it is **not under any open-source license**; owned by Fonix Corp., shared by a former developer in 2023. **[V]** https://github.com/RetroBunn/dt51

**Does any data survive?** Yes, in a legally awkward form: archive.org holds "Trope Trainer (Kinnor Software, Inc.)(Version 3.2)(2005)" as a 12.4 MB ISO (uploaded 2017-10-30, CD-ROM Preservation Project), and HN commenters report an activation code was posted there. Per *jes5199*, that ISO contains the XML trope tables and INI phoneme maps (older Hebrew synthesis than the final 2011 builds). **[V for the item; I for contents]** https://archive.org/details/Trope_Trainer_Kinnor_Software_Inc._Version_3.2_2005 . The source code is "probably gone" (donated computer); Schnee holds the only copy of the DECtalk voice engine she coded and declined to email it to the new TropeTrainer's programmer. **[V]** (Inverse). The 2011 Installation and User's Guide (r340, 7/6/11) exists on docplayer but the host failed DNS today. **[V-existence only]** https://docplayer.net/41371044-Tropetrainer-tm-software-installation-and-user-s-guide-c-2011-kinnor-software-inc-all-rights-reserved-r340-7-6-11.html . Wayback captures of kinnor.com could not be fetched from this environment.

**Buchler forum posts.** The leining Google Group has threads on its death and on a Chabad-adjusted trope tool ("simplified to modify"), but I found no post by Buchler explaining the melody tables. **[V]** https://groups.google.com/g/leining/c/xjW1BebOB28 , https://groups.google.com/g/leining/c/7d2ZhWx4UJI

**Patents.** None by Buchler or Kinnor found. Two related patents exist, both by **Norman Abramovitz and Jonathan Stiebel** (priority 2011): US 10,565,997 B1 (filed 2018-03-27, granted 2020-02-18; playback of a *human* chant recording between word-boundary times with synchronized highlighting; description mentions forced alignment) and US 11,380,334 B1 (filed 2019-10-21, granted 2022-07-05, assignee Intelligible English LLC; abstract: identify the cantillation symbols in a verse, "generate a chanting acoustic representation" of the ordered trope *names*, change its pitch level, play it). The second is the "chant the trope names" drill; it reads as recording-based, with a speech-synthesis CPC code attached. Expired-for-fees patents are unenforceable unless revived; flag for counsel only if we ever ship a "trope names" drill commercially. **[I]**

### 2.2 TropeTrainer by HazzanSolutions (2021–)

- Site today (© 2026): "6 voice styles" listed as Child, Soprano, Alto, Tenor, Baritone, Bass (the hero text names five); 4 playback modes (aliyah, verse, trope phrase, word); "8 speeds from 0.25x to 2x" (mockup shows six); 78+ voicing systems (Torah 32, Haftarah 25, High Holiday 6, Esther 6, Lamentations 3, Song of Songs 2, Ruth 2, Ecclesiastes 2); "6 accent traditions (Ashkenazi and Sephardi)"; word-level highlighting; standard-notation display; in-browser record-and-download; $13/mo or $130/yr; "Created under the guidance of an experienced Hazzan." **Nothing on the site says whether the voice is recorded or synthesized**, and there is no mention of AI. **[V]** https://www.tropetrainer.com/ , https://www.tropetrainer.com/features
- A third-party summary says users can adjust "chanting speed, pitch, accent, and vocal range." **[V-secondary]** (leining search snippet). Six "voice styles" that are vocal *ranges* (child through bass) applied to 78 voicing systems is not plausible as recorded MP3s per system per voice; it is almost certainly a synthesized or transposed engine. **[I]**
- In 2022 the app "has no voice" and the plan was cantors' MP3s; Friedman said it uses none of Buchler's code and registered the trademark in 2021. **[V]** (Inverse). HazzanSolutions' site lists TropeTrainer, iLeyn, Mitzvah Manager and Chant Torah America; no news, no engine statement. **[V]** https://hazzansolutions.com/ . Cantors Assembly partners on it. **[V]** https://www.cantors.org/tropetrainer/ . **No 2025–2026 news found.**
- Lesson: the paid incumbent competes on breadth of nusachot and vocal ranges, not on sounding like a specific cantor. "This specific cantor, this congregation's melody, free" is the differentiator; don't chase 78 voicing systems. **[I]**

## 3. Academic computational cantillation and Hebrew resources (2010–2026)

### 3.1 The two analysis papers

**van Kranenburg, Biró, Ness, Tzanetakis, ISMIR 2011, "A Computational Investigation of Melodic Contour Stability in Jewish Torah Trope Performance Traditions."** (The lead author is van Kranenburg; "Kranenburg et al." in the brief is this paper.) **[V]** (full text) https://ismir2011.ismir.net/papers/OS2-3.pdf
- Data: two recordings of the same Torah passage, one catalogued as Hungarian (the authors note it sounds Italian), one Moroccan; **manually segmented into te'amim by the domain expert** ("the task was too subjective and critical to automate"); ~130 realizations of each ta'am, 12 unique te'amim.
- Pitch: SWIPEP, 10 ms non-overlapping windows, converted to real-valued MIDI at 1-cent precision; a recording-specific scale from KDE peaks of the pitch histogram.
- Normalization: each contour scaled linearly by the recording's **two most prevalent pitches** (p_low = 0, p_high = 1), making performances at different absolute pitch comparable.
- Similarity: Needleman–Wunsch global alignment with S(x,y) = 1 − 4|sp_x − sp_y| if the difference ≤ 0.5 else −1, linear gap penalty 0.6, score normalized by the shorter sequence; this beat DTW on the earlier quantized-contour approach.
- Results (mean average precision per ta'am): Hungarian revia 0.994, etnachta 0.967, pashta 0.945, tipcha 0.683, katon 0.673; Moroccan much lower (katon 0.550, tipcha 0.399, pashta 0.299, etnachta 0.245). Ashkenazi signs are more stable per sign; tipcha is stable in both; conjunctive signs "might act as more stable syntactical anchors."

**Ben-Shalom, Keshet, Yeger-Granot, CIM14 (Berlin, 2014), "Automatic Analysis of Music: Performance of Cantillation Signs in Yemenite Jewish Traditional Cantillation."** (Hebrew University musicology and Bar-Ilan CS; Keshet is now at the Technion, which hosts the PDF.) **[V]** (full text) https://keshet.net.technion.ac.il/files/2022/05/BenShalomKeYe14.pdf
- Data: two Yemenite readers (Rabbi Shlomo Kare, Rabbi Shalom Cohen), 1955–1965 tapes from the Hebrew University Jewish Oral Traditions Research Center.
- Text side: a **rule-based converter from dotted (nikud) Hebrew to phonemes**, with look-ahead/behind rules and sonority rules for shva na/nach, judged "on a par with the manual phonemic transcription"; the te'amim positions are carried through, and since each Torah word has one accent, **word boundaries double as sign boundaries**.
- Acoustic side: a multiclass passive-aggressive frame classifier (39-dim MFCC, 25/10 ms) trained on **one manually aligned chapter (Genesis 5, 60/20/20 split)**, frame error 26.8%; then Keshet's discriminative phoneme aligner adapted to chant (new duration model, rate feature removed).
- Alignment accuracy on chant: 50.9% of boundaries within 10 ms, **66.1% within 20 ms, 73.3% within 30 ms, 79.2% within 40 ms, 83.2% within 50 ms, 85.9% within 60 ms.**
- Finding: the sof pasuq *finalis* (SWIPE, 10 ms) is not fixed; it drifts upward in "cycles" that line up with the textual sections of the chapter, so the reader segments meaning by pitch.
- Why it matters: this is a Hebrew Bible pipeline (nikud → phonemes → forced alignment on chant) with measured tolerances, bootstrapped from one chapter of hand labels. Our labeling plan (section 4) copies it.

### 3.2 Generation, recognition, datasets

- **No published system generates te'amim melodies**, and no 2024–2026 cantillation-recognition or pitch-feedback app surfaced. "Quantified Cantillation" (listed among Sefaria-powered projects) models trope-sequence transition probabilities, symbolic only. **[V]** https://developers.sefaria.org/docs/powered-by-sefaria . A 2020 academia.edu thesis works toward an intonation-based prosodic model of the Tiberian accents (not fetched). **[V-title]** https://www.academia.edu/44680073/
- **No Hebrew singing, cantillation or piyyut ML dataset exists** that I could find. General singing corpora (VocalSet, GTSinger, M4Singer, Opencpop, NUS-48E) are English/Mandarin/Japanese. **[V]**
- **Hebrew speech corpora:** ivrit.ai (3,300+ h, https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08720); HebDB (weakly supervised, CC BY 4.0, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.07566); VoxKnesset (~2,300 h, 393 speakers, 2009–2025, https://arxiv.org/html/2603.01270v1). **[V]**

## 4. Labeling and alignment of the cantor's minimal recordings

### 4.1 What "minimal" has to contain (so labeling is tractable)
For either engine path the recording must yield, per item: the Hebrew text with nikud and te'amim, a phone sequence, phone boundaries, and (for the SVS path) note boundaries and MIDI pitches. The drill set from the prior report (every haftarah trope motif on "la," on a 2- and a 3-syllable word, slow and in tempo, plus context variants and the haftarah final cadence, plus the blessings) is ~300 items / 30–45 min. Record each item as its own take with ≥1.5 s silence and a spoken slate; this makes step 1 below trivial and avoids Vāgdhenu's learned-pause problem. **[I]**

### 4.3 How much manual correction to expect
- Manual phone alignment **from scratch** is reported at "up to 130 times real-time" for speech. **[V]** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.00996
- After automatic alignment at ~75% of boundaries within 50 ms (the sung-English number), about one boundary in four needs a nudge. Hebrew chant at ~3–4 syllables/s is ~8 phones/s, so a 20 s verse has ~160 boundaries, ~40 to touch; at ~5 s per nudge that is 3–4 min per verse, i.e. **~10x real time for a careful phone-level pass**, ~3–5x for a word-level pass, plus a listening check. For the 30–45 min drill set: **5–8 hours** of correction; for a 7 min weekly haftarah: ~1 hour phone-level or ~20 min word-level. **[I]**
- Expect the systematic error, not random error: long sung vowels truncated (ratio ~0.53 for >210 ms phones), short consonants inflated. A duration prior per phone class (Vāgdhenu's "duration gates") and snapping vowel ends to the energy/pitch offset will fix most of these automatically. **[I from V]**
- Hand-labeled seed: ~10 minutes of audio (≈30 verses or ≈100 drill items) is in line with CIM14's one chapter. Budget **3–4 hours** for the seed at ~20x real time, done once.

### 4.4 Recommended labeling workflow (concrete)
1. **Text side first.** From the sheet's Masoretic text: rule G2P (nikud, dagesh, meteg, shva na/nach, final forms) to a Hebrew phone set of ~35 symbols, parameterized by the cantor's pronunciation choices (question 2 below). Emit per word: syllables, stressed syllable, the ta'am, and the expected motif id from the trope-grammar agent's tables. Unit-test the G2P on 50 words the cantor has pronounced.
2. **Take management.** Silence-split (`ffmpeg silencedetect`) into items; verify each item's text with ivrit.ai Whisper (accept if CER < 10% after nikud stripping; otherwise re-slate). Loudness-normalize (-16 LUFS) but keep the 48 kHz/24-bit masters.
3. **Zero-training pass.** `ctc-forced-aligner --language heb --romanize --split_size char` for word and char boundaries; convert to a first-draft phone TextGrid using the G2P phone sequence (char→phone is near one-to-one once nikud is expanded). Record the NC license of the default weights in the decisions log.
4. **Seed set.** Hand-correct ~100 drill items or ~30 verses in Praat/vLabeler from the draft; mark note boundaries where a syllable carries more than one note (melisma). This set is also the **evaluation gold** and must never be used for training the final aligner's test metrics (hold out 20%).
5. **Train HubertFA (or SOFA)** with the seed as `full_label` and everything else as `weak_label`; report BER at 20/50 ms on the held-out 20%. Iterate twice: align all → correct the lowest-confidence 20% → retrain (MFA-singing paper's idea). Stop when ≥ 85% within 50 ms on the hold-out, which matches CIM14.
6. **Pitch and notes (SVS path only).** RMVPE or SwiftF0 f0 at 10 ms; SOME or a simple rule (f0 plateau ≥ 60 ms within ±50 cents) for note segmentation; write DiffSinger `transcriptions.csv` (`ph_seq, ph_dur, note_seq, note_dur`). For the concatenation path, store per-syllable units with their f0 contour and the motif id instead.
7. **QA gates, automatic:** per-phone duration within class bounds; every item's phone count equals the G2P's; melisma syllables have ≥ 2 notes; no item with > 2 s unlabeled audio. **QA gate, human:** the cantor listens to the auto-sliced motif audio for 20 random items per session (5 minutes) and flags wrong cuts.
8. **Deliverables:** `labels/<item>.TextGrid`, `labels/<item>.csv`, a `labels/manifest.json` with aligner version, confidence, and reviewer; all under version control, since the labels are the durable asset after the WAVs.

## 5. Evaluation

### 5.1 Pitch extraction (the measuring instrument)
Independent benchmark (lars76/pitch-benchmark v2.1, pitch F1 at 50 cents, 10 corpora, 8 degradations, 16 ms grid): SwiftF0 0.781 (180x real time), **RMVPE 0.768** (13.6x), FCPE 0.728 (27.8x), TorchCREPE 0.691 (0.4x), PESTO 0.680 (20x), Praat 0.651, **pYIN 0.506** (9.6x). SwiftF0 vs RMVPE is "not statistically resolved." **[V]** https://github.com/lars76/pitch-benchmark . On clean vocals (MIR-1K) CREPE's raw pitch accuracy is ~95.7% vs pYIN ~74.7% (RMVPE paper, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.15412); FCPE reports ~96.8% RPA and 5.3x RMVPE's speed (https://www.researchgate.net/publication/395648769). **[V]**
Recommendation: RMVPE (or SwiftF0) as the primary tracker, FCPE as a cheap second opinion; disagreement > 50 cents on > 5% of voiced frames flags a bad render. Avoid pYIN for anything but a sanity plot. Check each tracker's license before vendoring (the benchmark doesn't list them). **[I]**

### 5.2 Melodic correctness (does it sing the right trope?)
- **Representation.** f0 → cents → per-syllable contour; normalize by the cantor's two structural pitches (reciting tone and final tone) exactly as ISMIR 2011 did, so his key on a given day doesn't matter. Build the *expected* contour for a verse from the drill-set motifs (the engine's own target) or, better, from his real recording of that verse when one exists.
- **Score.** Needleman–Wunsch with the ISMIR similarity (S = 1 − 4|Δ| for |Δ| ≤ 0.5, else −1; gap 0.6; normalized by the shorter sequence), which outperformed DTW on trope contours. Also report frame-level RPA/RCA/overall accuracy via `mir_eval.melody` against the expected track (standard MIREX metrics). **[I from V]** https://mir-eval.readthedocs.io/
- **Note-level check (the hard gate).** Segment the render into notes, map to scale degrees relative to the reciting tone, and compute edit distance to the motif's skeletal note sequence. Skeleton errors = fail; ornament-count differences = warn (TENOR 2024's skeletal vs ornamental distinction).
- **Calibration.** Have the cantor record 10 verses **twice**; the distribution of take-to-take distances is the "human variance band." A render passes if its distance to the expected contour is within the 90th percentile of that band (ISMIR's within-ta'am distance histograms are the model). Without this calibration any threshold is arbitrary.

### 5.3 Does it sound like him? (speaker similarity)
- Metric: cosine similarity of speaker embeddings. Singing SVS papers use **WavLM fine-tuned for speaker verification** (TCSinger, TCSinger 2); TTS work finds ECAPA-TDNN beats x-vector; Resemblyzer is GE2E. **[V]** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.14910 , https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.10473
- Pitfalls (SSW 2025 analysis): embeddings mostly encode static traits (mean pitch, HNR, shimmer) and poorly capture rhythm; **duration mismatch** between reference and test shifts scores (same-speaker EER fell to 0.30–0.39); **EQ differences** are "catastrophic" for GE2E; noise at 0 dB SNR breaks all of them. Protocol: match durations, use the same texts, measure SNR on both sides, re-equalize renders to the reference's spectral balance, and report EER against two controls: same-speaker (should be ~50%) and different-speaker (should be ~0%). **[V]** https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02176 . Perceptual similarity datasets exist (VoxSim) showing cosine often diverges from human judgment. **[V]** https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2024/ahn24b_interspeech.pdf
- For us: positives = held-out real recordings of the cantor; negatives = PocketTorah's CC BY-SA readers chanting similar verses (free, same genre). Report the render's cosine relative to those two clouds, not an absolute number. Because the verification models are speech-trained, treat this as a trend indicator between engine versions, not proof of identity. **[I]**

### 5.4 Listening tests
- **Automatic MOS predictors:** speech MOS models (UTMOS, DNSMOS) "transfer poorly to singing"; on SingMOS-Pro (7,981 clips, 11.15 h, Mandarin/Japanese, 78 annotators) an SSL model with a pitch-histogram feature was best (system-level LCC 0.88, SRCC 0.79). Nothing is Hebrew or chant; use at most as a trend line. **[V]** https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01812v4 , https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.10911
- **Cantor blind review protocol** (two sessions of ≤ 30 min per engine version):
  1. 20 verses, half real recordings, half renders, shuffled; for each: the Hebrew with trope colors, the current word highlighted, and (toggleable) the expected motif-name sequence.
  2. **ABX** on 10 pairs (real A, render B, X one of them): measures whether he can tell. Report % correct; 50% means indistinguishable.
  3. **Pass/fail per verse with reason codes:** wrong motif; motif on wrong syllable; pronunciation; phrasing/pause; timbre not me; artifact; "would teach a learner something wrong." The last code is the publication gate: zero tolerated.
  4. His verdicts on the *real* recordings are the control for his own false-fail rate; if he fails his own takes > 10% of the time, loosen the stylistic codes, not the melody codes.
  5. Sign-off rule for public release: ≥ 95% melody-pass on the regression set, 0 "would teach wrong," and timbre-pass ≥ 80% (the timbre bar is his to set; question 4).
- **Class listening (MOS-style):** 5-point naturalness and 5-point "sounds like Cantor Josh" on 10 items with a hidden reference (his real take) and a low anchor (the concatenation render); CMOS pairwise vs the real take. Keep each session under 10 minutes; 15–20 listeners is enough to rank engine versions. **[I]** (ITU-T P.800 / ITU-R BS.1534 MUSHRA conventions.)

### 5.5 Regression suite (runs on every engine change)
- **Fixed set:** ~30 verses chosen so every haftarah trope mark and every context rule occurs at least twice (pashta doubling, munach before zarka, short and long tevir, telisha, prepositive/postpositive stress, the haftarah final cadence), plus 5 verses that have golden real recordings and the 10 twice-recorded calibration verses.
- **Checks, all automatic, all CPU:** (a) note-sequence edit distance to the computed skeleton = 0 (hard fail); (b) contour alignment distance within the calibrated band (hard fail); (c) f0 RPA vs the previous golden render > 95% unless the change is intended (warn; prevents silent drift); (d) speaker cosine vs the cantor cloud not lower than the last release by more than 0.03 (warn); (e) phone durations within class bounds, G2P phone count matches, no clipping, loudness within ±1 LU (hard fail); (f) runtime and file size.
- **Mechanics:** render into `renders/<git-sha>/`, write a JSON report per verse, generate a one-page HTML diff with A/B players for anything that changed, and only promote a render to "golden" after the cantor's sign-off. pytest plus a GitHub Actions job (CPU) is sufficient; RMVPE on 30 verses is seconds. **[I]**
